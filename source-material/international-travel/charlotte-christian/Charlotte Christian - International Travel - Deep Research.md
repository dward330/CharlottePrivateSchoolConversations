## Opening summary

Charlotte Christian School does not run a branded "global education" program, global
diploma, or international exchange. Its student international travel happens through
**Winterim**, a required one-week Upper School (grades 9–12) term that includes
"domestic, and international opportunities". The school's global learning is housed under a
Diversity & Belonging office whose director carries the title "Director of Diversity,
Belonging and Global Awareness." Trip prices are published as ranges in a Winterim packet.
However, no trip-specific financial aid, travel endowment, exchange partner, Round Square
membership or global credential appears anywhere on the official site.

## TL;DR

- **What exists:** International student travel is offered only in Upper School, through
  Winterim, plus occasional summer trips. Winterim is "Required for all students • .25
  credit", but going abroad is one option among on-campus, local and domestic choices.
  Official pages document trips to Peru, Iceland, Greece, Italy, Spain and the Dominican
  Republic. The Dominican Republic trips are run with SCORE International, which is the
  school's longest-running documented travel partner, on record since 2009-10. On-campus
  global learning runs through a Lower School Multicultural Festival, a "Global Knights"
  student group, a "Global Awareness Prefect" and world-language study.
- **What's missing:** The site publishes no global diploma or certificate, no exchange or
  sister schools, no Round Square or similar network membership, and no count of how many
  students travel each year. No Middle or Lower School international travel is published.
- **The money:** Trip costs run from "$200-250" for local Charlotte experiences to
  "$4,650-5,045" for the top-priced travel experience in the 2026 Winterim packet (the PDF
  now returns 404). The packet says "The advertised cost is subject to change". Need-based
  aid is tuition-only: "Financial assistance grants will not exceed 50 percent of tuition.
  Parents are responsible for the remaining tuition balance and all fees." The site
  publishes no travel scholarship, named fund or endowment for global education.

## 11. Gaps & questions to ask the school

1. **Starting grade.** Can a 9th grader choose an international Winterim trip, or do priority rules
   ("highest grade level available") effectively reserve international trips for juniors and
   seniors?
2. **Current trip list.** What destinations, dates, leaders and prices are in the 2027 Winterim
   Packet?
3. **Cost.** The 2026 packet states: "All costs include student activities, tickets or entrance fees
   and local transportation. Costs for overnight trips also include most student meals,
   accommodations and air or bus fare." Which meals and extras (insurance, tips) are not covered,
   and how far can the final price move from the advertised range?
4. **Aid.** Does any aid, scholarship or named fund cover Winterim travel for families who get
   need-based tuition aid?
5. **Selection.** How are oversubscribed trips allocated, and what does "demonstrated readiness"
   mean?
6. **Safety.** What are the chaperone ratios, medical and emergency protocols, and State Department
   advisory policy? Does the school carry its own travel insurance?
7. **Exchanges.** Are there any partner or sister schools, or reciprocal exchanges with homestays?
8. **Credential.** Is a global certificate, or a transcript notation for Winterim or the Seal of
   Biliteracy, offered or planned?
9. **Scale.** How many students travel internationally each year, and what share of Winterim
   participants is that?
10. **Middle and Lower School.** Are there any plans for international travel or virtual exchange
    before grade 9?
11. **Networks.** Does the school belong to Round Square or any similar network, and which Model UN
    conferences does it attend?
12. **Credit.** Is the Winterim graduation requirement 1 unit or 0.25 per year?
