Program Highlights
Switzerland becomes a living classroom in this immersive travel program, where students explore the dynamic intersection of science, technology, and global collaboration. Through visits to world-renowned institutions such as CERN and Technorama, students engage in hands-on workshops and interactive exhibits that bring core STEM concepts to life, deepening their understanding of physics, earth science, engineering, and design thinking.
Throughout the program, students encounter real-world applications of science and innovation. They learn how global institutions collaborate to advance human knowledge, how sustainability and efficiency shape Swiss infrastructure, and how legendary thinkers like Einstein were shaped by their environment. Emphasis is placed not only on scientific exploration but also on how technology and cooperation drive progress across borders.
This journey goes beyond sightseeing. It is a structured learning experience designed to spark curiosity, foster critical thinking, and inspire students to see science as an evolving force with real-world impact.
TENTATIVE Switzerland Itinerary (Subject to change)
Day 1
After an overnight flight, we arrive in Zurich. In the afternoon, we explore Zurich’s Old Town through the lens of Albert Einstein’s life and legacy. We navigate the cobblestone streets, participating in an activity that transforms the historic city into an interactive learning experience, blending team-building challenges and puzzles with stories from Einstein’s years in Zurich as a student and professor. We then travel to visit the FIFA Museum before ending the day with dinner at a local restaurant.
Day 2
We travel by train to Geneva. First is a visit to Addictlab Academy for an afternoon of interactive STEM-based innovation, where we participate in hands-on activities exploring the intersections of science, art, and creativity. We then travel to explore Geneva’s international district, exploring the outside of sites such as the United Nations, World Health Organization, and International Red Cross. An activity helps us consider the challenges of collaborative global decision-making.
Day 3
We start the day with a visit to CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research and one of the world’s foremost centers for scientific discovery. Here, (pending availability), we take a tour and engage with interactive exhibitions that illuminate the mysteries of the universe, from particle collisions to the origins of matter, while gaining insight into how international collaboration fuels groundbreaking research. Next is the Patek Philippe Museum, where a guided tour on the art of watchmaking offers a fascinating perspective on precision, mechanics, and time. Afterward, we travel by train to Bern.
Day 4
Day four begins with a visit to the Bern Historical Museum, where we learn more about Einstein’s life and work and engage with the multi-sensory exhibits. We then return to Zurich where we visit the Lindt Home of Chocolate. Here, we participate in a guided tour that dives into the history and innovation behind Swiss chocolate. We also take the time to reflect on the science involved in this sweet creation.
Day 5
On our final day,we transfer to Winterthur to spend the day at the Swiss Science Center Technorama. Here, we immerse ourselves in interactive exhibits that bring physics, chemistry, and engineering concepts to life. After lunch at the Science Center, we participate in a hands-on lab that gives us another opportunity to experience the wonders of science. The day concludes with a celebratory dinner and our program closing where we take time to reflect on our journey, share key learnings, and express gratitude for one another’s contributions.
Day 6
After breakfast, we gather for our final reflection before transferring to the airport for our flight back to Charlotte.
SCIENCE AS COLLABORATION
Switzerland plays a unique role in the global scientific landscape, serving as home to institutions like CERN, where breakthroughs in particle physics are only possible through international cooperation. Students explore how large-scale scientific research depends on shared resources, open communication, and the combined efforts of diverse teams across borders. This lens encourages students to consider science not just as a body of knowledge, but as a collective, human endeavor shaped by diplomacy, ethics, and global trust.
PRECISION, DESIGN, AND THE POWER OF CURIOSITY
From watchmaking to robotics, Switzerland is a hub of high-precision thinking and applied innovation. This lens challenges students to approach learning with curiosity, attention to detail, and a willingness to prototype and problem-solve. Throughout the program, students encounter STEM not as a static theory but as a toolkit for exploring and shaping the world. In doing so, they engage with design thinking and creative resilience as essential elements of scientific inquiry.
SCIENCE IN CONTEXT: HISTORY, ETHICS, AND ENVIRONMENT
Science is shaped by the world around it. Throughout the journey, students consider how place, time, and ethics inform discovery, whether tracing Einstein’s steps in Bern or learning how Swiss infrastructure reflects sustainability and public well-being. This lens asks students to think critically about the responsibilities that come with knowledge and to explore how scientific advancement connects to broader questions of peace, policy, and the future of our planet.
participation requirements
All students must be in good academic and behavioral standing to travel on a PDS Global Trip. If at any time a student is on academic or behavioral probation before the trip departs, PDS reserves the right to remove the student from the trip.
For international travel, students must have a current, valid passport with an expiration date that is at least six months beyond the return date of the trip.
Students are expected to be open to new cultural experiences, such as sampling new or different types of cuisines and meeting people they typically would not have the opportunity to interact with. This may, at times, push students out of their comfort zone. These situations can help students gain global empathy, confidence, and understanding.
Students must attend all pre-departure meetings.
A single student in a hotel room is not permissible.
