## 1a. Arts Ladder (division by division)

### Lower School (JrK–4)

- Visual art is **choice-based / Teaching for Artistic Behavior (TAB)**. Lower School students
  explore "drawing, painting, collage, paper sculpture, clay and fiber art" and work "like practicing
  artists" with autonomy over creative direction. **[VERIFIED]**
  https://www.cannonschool.org/arts/visual-arts
- Music in the younger grades is built on the **Orff approach**, expanding into "advanced ensembles"
  in later divisions. **[VERIFIED]** https://www.cannonschool.org/arts
- TAB is described as implemented **across all divisions since 2016–2017**. **[VERIFIED]**
  https://www.cannonschool.org/arts/visual-arts
- **TAB pilot year CORRECTED — it was 2011, not 2015–16, and it began in the Lower School.**
  **Krista Johns** "helped implement and pilot TAB in the Lower School in **2011**" after noticing
  "that students at different artistic skill levels became frustrated with prescribed lessons."
  **"Once the success of the program in Lower School became clear, Middle and Upper School adopted the
  teaching method."** **[VERIFIED]** https://www.cannonschool.org/artistic-visionaries
  - Her own framing of why: *"In a traditional art class, you hold up a piece of artwork and you ask
    students to copy what you've done… But not every student has the fine motor skills to do that."*
    And on the result: *"When you're creating work that's based on your own experiences and interests,
    and you're not comparing yourself to anyone next to you, you're more willing to try new things."*
  - **This is a strong, quotable provenance story: TAB at Cannon is teacher-originated and
    ~15 years deep, not an imported trend.** Use the 2011 date.

- Choice-based visual art continues, with "drawing, painting, printmaking, ceramics, and sculpture."

  https://www.cannonschool.org/artistic-visionaries — a real on-ramp years before Upper School CTC.
  **Title/year [NOT FOUND].**

  - Visual Art (TAB model)
  - **Photography and Tech Art Fusion**
  - **Creative Explorations** (multidisciplinary writing + visual arts)
  - **Arts Magazine** (production-focused — this is the student arts publication)
### The "Varsity" ensembles — CORRECTED: co-curricular, before school, 7:30 a.m. **[VERIFIED]**

The baseline treated Varsity Band/Chorus/Orchestra as courses. They are **not** — they are
**before-school co-curricular ensembles** for students who want arts "beyond their class schedule,"
which is why they appear nowhere in the course catalog. Source:
https://www.cannonschool.org/artistic-visionaries

- **"Starting at 7:30 a.m., students gather for Varsity Chorus, Band, and Orchestra, ensembles offered
  to musicians who want arts experiences beyond their class schedule."**
- **Varsity Chorus** — **founded by Rob Burlington**, Director of Music Arts; **auditioned**;
  **"There are 40 students in the Varsity Chorus this year."** Meets **"early in the morning a few
  times a week"** and rehearses **"music across genres."** Rationale: students "may have had class
  conflicts that prevented them from taking chorus during the day… or they may simply want more
  opportunities to create."
- **Varsity Orchestra and Varsity Band** — both created *after* Varsity Chorus, because of its
  popularity: **"The group's popularity led to the creation of Varsity Orchestra and Band, both of
  which also rehearse outside class hours."**
- **Varsity Band** spans **grades 6–12** and meets before school; **Varsity Orchestra** is
  **competitive — Middle and Upper School students audition for seats and "have to work to maintain
  them."** **[SCHOOL-CLAIM — from Cannon's music page summary; the 7:30 a.m. co-curricular framing is
  VERIFIED]**
- **Why this matters for the card:** these ensembles are the **access-and-depth story**. A student
  whose schedule is full of AP courses can still sing in an auditioned 40-person chorus, and a 6th
  grader can play alongside 12th graders. That is a genuine structural advantage — but it costs a
  7:30 a.m. commitment, which parents should know.

- **Cindric Performing Arts Center (CPAC)** — **"Built in 2014 and fully funded by gifts from more than

  - **Hardwood, painted black** "to facilitate lighting and staging"; the wood floor "allows for the

  - **Structural grid** over the stage "that allows for changing theatrical sets or for re-configuring
    curtains."
  - **Catwalk above the stage** — and notably it "gives **students** the ability to assist on rigging
    lighting for different stage events."
  - **Backstage ramp** doubling as a corridor so actors can cross to stage right/left mid-performance;

  - **Boom speakers tucked underneath the stage** for sound projection and special effects.
- **Student-run control room CONFIRMED:** "a state-of-the-art AV system which includes a **control room

## 1e. Verdict

### Why the program holds up (concrete, sourced)

1. **School-wide choice-based art (TAB) is a genuine, rare structural commitment — not a slogan.**
   TAB runs JrK–12, adopted school-wide in 2016–17, and the division descriptions actually reflect it
   (students "make choices about what they want to create," working "like practicing artists").
   Most peer schools run teacher-directed projects with a choice unit; Cannon inverted the default.
   **[VERIFIED]** https://www.cannonschool.org/arts/visual-arts
2. **Creative Design & Engineering is a three-level arts-side engineering track.** CAD, metalworking,
   electronics, coding, laser cutting, CNC milling and textiles inside the *arts* department gives a
   maker-inclined kid a four-year path that isn't STEM-club-shaped. Few K–12 arts departments own CNC
   and laser equipment across a sequenced I–III track. **[VERIFIED]**
3. **Theatre has hard, third-party external validation.** Ten Blumey finalist nominations across two
   documented years (5 in 2023 for *Freaky Friday*, 5 in 2025 for *Mamma Mia!*) in all five major
   performance categories each time — Best Actor, Best Actress, Supporting Actor, Supporting Actress,
   Featured Performer. Independently verified in Blumenthal's own nominee PDFs, not school PR.
   **[VERIFIED]**
4. **The theatre season is structurally serious: three distinct outward-facing commitments a year** —
   NCTC festival play in the fall, Blumey-competing musical in the winter, spring show — plus a May
   Cabaret with awards. A student can perform, compete, and be adjudicated every season.
   **[VERIFIED]**
5. **Facilities are real and recently reinvested in.** CPAC (11,000+ sq ft, 450-seat configurable
   house), Campbell Gallery, and the **newly opened Texas Grace Audio Lab** for sound engineering and
   music production — the audio lab shows the arts plant is still being funded, not coasting.
   **[VERIFIED]**
6. **The entire Upper School arts catalog is Honors/AP/Advanced Topics — 22 courses, zero regular
   track — behind a two-year arts graduation requirement.** Arts courses carry the same transcript
   weight as academic honors courses, and every student must take two years. That combination tells you
   the arts are not treated as a soft elective. **[VERIFIED — catalog]**
7. **There are four genuinely deep four-year ladders, not one.** Strings runs **Honors String Ensemble
   I–IV**; music production runs **Music Tech I → II → III**; studio runs **Visual Foundations →
   Studio-2D/3D → Studio Arts II → III → AP** (with **AT Art History** alongside); CDE runs **I → II →
   Studio**. A committed student in any of four different arts identities has somewhere to go for four
   years. **[VERIFIED — catalog]**
8. **Honors Theater in Performance can be entered with three CTC productions instead of the course
   prerequisite,** and is repeatable for credit — the after-school company and the classroom are
   formally interoperable. **[VERIFIED — catalog]**

### Honest counterweights (for the parent, and for card design)

- **Music and visual arts show no documented external-honors pipeline** (no All-State/All-District,
  no Tri-M, no Scholastic Art). Theatre carries the external-recognition load.
- **No published NCTC results ledger** — participation is certain, outcomes are unknown.
- **No arts faculty named publicly** except the theater director, which limits pre-tour vetting.
- **The Media Arts pathway does not appear in the current course catalog** despite being promoted on
  the website and cited as an AP prerequisite. If a family is choosing Cannon *for* film/graphic
  design/video, this must be confirmed live — it is the biggest soft spot in the offering.
- **The website and the catalog disagree** on several course names ("Acting 1," "Stagecraft," "Studio
  Arts I," "Media Arts I"). The public arts pages appear to be out of date relative to the catalog,
  which is worth knowing before relying on any website course list.

### Sharp tour questions

1. "TAB is school-wide — so how do you get a kid from open-ended choice in Lower School to a
   disciplined **AP Studio Art portfolio** by senior year? Where does technical skill instruction get
   inserted?" (The central tension in any choice-based program.)
2. "**How did the NCTC festival play place** the last three falls — did you advance out of regionals
   to the state festival, and did students take individual acting or technical awards?" (Fills the
   biggest gap; also tests whether the program measures itself.)
3. "Ten Blumey finalist nominations in 2023 and 2025 but I couldn't find a **win** — how many Blumey
   awards has Cannon actually taken home, and how many kids audition for the musical each year?"
   (Depth vs. star-driven.)
4. "For music: are Cannon students selected for **All-District/All-State or NCAIS honors ensembles**,
   and does the Jazz Combo play adjudicated festivals or outside gigs? Roughly how many per year?"
   (Directly probes the documented gap.)
5. "In **CDE**, what does a student actually finish with by **CDE-Studio** — and is the CNC/laser
   equipment student-operated or staff-operated?" (Separates a real shop from a display shop.)
6. "**Is Media Arts still running?** Your website promotes Media Arts & Productions and a Senior
   Seminar nonprofit project, and AP Studio Art lists 'Media Arts I' as a prerequisite — but I don't
   see any Media Arts course in the Upper School catalog. Which courses exist this year, and can I see
   two recent finished nonprofit deliverables?" (**The highest-value question on this list** — a
   documented contradiction, asked precisely.)
7. "Is **CAFTA the same thing as 'Parents at Cannon Create'**, and what does the parent arts group
   actually fund in a typical year?" (Resolves the naming conflict found in the sources.)
8. "Every arts course is labeled Honors or AP. **How is an Honors arts course weighted in GPA**, and
   what distinguishes honors-level expectations in, say, Concert Chorus from a non-honors chorus
   elsewhere?" (Tests whether the honors labeling is substantive or nominal.)
9. "**AP Studio Art lists 'Studio Arts I'** as a prerequisite but the catalog's courses are Studio-2D
   and Studio-3D — which ones actually let my child sit for the 2-D portfolio versus the 3-D or
   Drawing portfolio?" (Resolves the catalog's internal inconsistency.)
10. "Jazz Combo requires **'at least one year of Jazz 1'** — where is Jazz 1 taught, since it isn't in
    the Upper School catalog? Can a 9th grader who arrives playing well get into the Combo?"
