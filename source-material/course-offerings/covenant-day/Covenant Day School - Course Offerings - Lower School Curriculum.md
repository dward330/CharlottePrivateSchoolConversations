## Junior Kindergarten

*(tile `post_id=666`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Phonics: Demonstrate directionality (left-right), read all individual letters of the alphabet by saying the sounds correctly (phonics), read at least 10 digraphs, identify initial, end and medial sounds of words, read short vowel words using phonetic knowledge by sound blending (CVC), and read at least 5 common exception (sight) words
Reading Comprehension: Demonstrate understanding by answering and asking questions about a text, predict what might happen next in a story, retell stories in the correct sequence 
Writing: Write most lowercase and capital letters using correct letter formation and write name using correct letter formation
Math
Shapes and Measurement:
Understand positional relationships
Identify and describe 2D shapes
Compare length, weight, and capacity using appropriate vocabulary
Number Sense:
Verbally count confidently to 20
Count out loud backward from 10 by 1s 
Count up to 10 objects/Cardinal Principle
Automatically recall number bonds to 5
Automatically recall double facts to 10
Subitize to 5
Write numbers 1-10 using the correct formation
Patterns:
Identify and extend repeating patterns
Sort and classify by attributes (color, shape, size)
Compare quantities (<, >, =) up to 10
Bible
Students learn about God's love. Students memorize short Scripture passages and learn about key Bible characters and their stories. They also attend chapel every week and participate in lower school Missions Week.
Science and Social Studies
Students confidently explore the natural world, making appropriate comments and observations. They learn the names of parts of the body and important processes and changes in the world around them (seasons, life cycles, plant growth, and changing states of matter.) They also learn about significant historical figures, understand past, present, and future concepts, similarities and differences between life in their country and life in other countries, and appreciate community helpers.
Art
Students use their imagination to role-play activities and perform for an audience. Nursery rhymes and songs are a part of learning, along with various materials, tools, and art techniques.

## Kindergarten

*(tile `post_id=667`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Phonics: Phonological awareness, short vowel words, and sight word recognition
Reading Comprehension: Story retelling, listening comprehension,  verbal reasoning and background knowledge
Writing: Handwriting, sentence structure and punctuation, forming sentences, and writing stories
Math
Using manipulatives, students engage in math talks, counting and skip counting to 100, number recognition and formation from 0-20, place value to 20, geometry, patterns sorting, and classifying. Students learn concepts of measurements, adding and subtracting combinations of 10, graphing, estimating, and calendar components.
Bible
Students learn about friendship with God and others through group projects, activity centers, and community life in the classroom. They attend chapel each week and participate in the lower school Missions Week. Students memorize short Scripture passages and discover Bible characters and their stories.
Science
Students discover God’s awesome strength in the oceans, creative patterns through the seasons of the year, and animal life cycles.
Social Studies
Students learn about God's sovereign plan for the lives of people through the explorers, the pilgrims, and Native Americans and about His creativity through famous artists and holidays. Through a study of world cultures and traditions, students learn about empathy for others in light of the Gospel.

## First Grade

*(tile `post_id=668`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Phonics/Spelling: Phonological awareness, decoding, sight word recognition, fluency, and vocabulary.
Grammar: Parts of Speech: nouns (common, proper, singular, plural), verbs, adjectives, capitalization, punctuation and sentence types.
Reading Comprehension: Retelling, summarizing, visualizing, verbalizing, main idea and details, compare/contrast, cause/effect, classify/categorize, and fact/opinion.
Writing: Personal narratives, opinion, informational, and letter formation (printing).
Math
Using manipulatives, students learn place value to 100, numbers to 120, number bonds and tasks, addition and subtraction to 100, ordinal numbers, and position words. Students explore 2D and 3D shapes and patterns, mental math, customary measurement: length and weight, and math vocabulary. Students are introduced to picture and bar graphs and money and expand their knowledge of calendar and time skills.
Bible
Students study God’s truths through the lives of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, Joseph, Moses, the nation of Israel, and the settling of the Promised Land. Other Bible stories include Judges and Ruth, Samuel and Saul, King David and Solomon’s reigns, the divided kingdom, exile and return of Israel, Psalms, and Proverbs.
Science
Students examine seasonal patterns, plants, light and sound waves, animal behaviors, and habitats.
Social Studies
Students learn safety and laws and about responsible citizenship. Students engage in units about home, school and community, and map skills. They also gain an understanding of past, present, and future events.

## Second Grade

*(tile `post_id=669`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Phonics/Spelling: Phonological awareness, decoding and encoding short and long vowel words and multi-syllabic words, sight word recognition, fluency, and vocabulary
Grammar: Parts of Speech (nouns-common, proper, singular, plural), verbs, adjectives; capitalization and punctuation, sentence types and contractions
Reading Comprehension: Retelling, summarizing, visualizing and verbalizing, sequencing, main ideas and details, compare/contrast, cause/effect, classify/categorize, fact/opinion, language structure, verbal reasoning, and background knowledge to the text
Writing: Expanding personal narratives, opinion, information, and letter formation (printing and cursive)
Math
Students build on previous math experiences, strengthen number sense, fact families to 20, mental math and estimation, place value up to 1,000, and adding and subtracting 2 and 3 digit numbers. Students use math manipulatives throughout math units, including the introduction of fractions, multiplication and division, time and elapsed time, money, and customary and metric measurements. Students explore graphs, charts, line plots, and problem-solving strategies using word problems and math tools with bar models and picture representation.
Bible
Students review the Old Testament with the Jesse Tree and the inter testament time period.  The focus is on the New Testament, including Jesus’ birth, ministry, preaching, praying, miracles, parables, and the crucifixion and resurrection of Christ. Studies conclude with the early church and Paul’s ministry and missionary journeys.
Science
Students explore the human body, life cycles, insects, oceanography, animal behaviors, and interactions with matter.
Social Studies
Students study immigration, geography’s influence on communities, community helpers, globes and map skills, community resources and stewardship, holidays, and culture across the world. They also learn about how government officials are elected and how history contributes to a community’s heritage.

## Third Grade

*(tile `post_id=670`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Spelling: Encoding and decoding, patterns and rules, and syllable division.
Grammar: Identify relationships among words, including synonyms, antonyms, homonyms, and multiple-meaning words, contractions, effective use of capitalization, punctuation, spelling, adjectives/adverbs, prepositions, regular and irregular verbs, abstract nouns/pronouns, build and use accurately conversational, general academic, and content-specific words and phrases.
Reading Comprehension: Predicting, visualizing and verbalizing, summarizing, sequencing, inferring and drawing conclusions; compare/contrast, main ideas and details; cause/effect; author’s purpose, story elements, reading accuracy, fluency and expression, vocabulary and language structure.
Writing: Complete simple, compound and declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences, using coordinating and subordinating conjunctions and interjections; classify and identify parts of a sentence and apply in composition (subject, verb, capital letter, end mark, makes complete sense), differentiate between a complete, sentence fragment, and run on sentence.
Math
Students learn place value to 10,000, rounding numbers, mental math and estimation, addition and subtraction to 10.000, bar models to solve word problems, deepen number sense, multiplication facts 0-12, multiplication and division of 1 and 2 digit numbers, fractions and decimals, angles and lines, area and perimeter, 3D and 2D shapes. Students expand their knowledge of money, time and elapsed time, customary metric measurement conversions, bar graphs, and line plots.
Bible
Students study creation, the fall, Noah, the Tower of Babel, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, the Exodus, the ten commandments, wandering in the wilderness, and the Promised Land. The year concludes with a Bible book study of Ephesians.
Science
Students learn about forces and magnetism, the earth and the activity on it, plants, animals, and fossils.
Social Studies
Students discover how events, individuals, and ideas have influenced communities, especially Charlotte, NC. Students learn about major landforms and their impact on people, industries, the economy, and the 13 American colonies.

## Fourth Grade

*(tile `post_id=671`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Spelling: Decoding and reading words by applying phonics and word analysis skills, fluency and accuracy when reading, building and applying vocabulary using various strategies and sources, applying knowledge of word structure elements, determining the meanings, pronunciations, syllabication, synonyms, antonyms, and parts of speech of words by using reference aids as glossaries, dictionaries, and thesauruses
Grammar: Morphology, spelling rules (suffixes, prefixes, base words, contractions, compound words, and syllables), types of sentences, punctuation, singular and possessive nouns, titles and quotation marks, parts of a sentence (subject, predicate, conjunctions, nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositional phrases, subject-verb agreement), editing/revising, styles of writing (informational, poetry, narrative, summary, creative, persuasive, literary analysis, author studies)
Reading Comprehension: Visualization and verbalization, vocabulary, verbal reasoning, language structure, summarizing, making corrections, inferring and predicting, asking questions, fact/opinion, author’s viewpoint, and character development and reading and comprehending a variety of literature independently and proficiently
Writing: Develop writing skills by writing for different purposes and to specific audiences and people (persuasive, informative, narrative, personal), produce coherent and legible documents by planning, drafting, revising, editing, and collaborating with others
Math
Students continue to build on math concepts, including number sense, addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division (facts, multi-digit numbers, common multiples, factors, estimation, and problem solving. Students master skills in the areas of mean, median, mode and range, fractions (adding and subtracting, equivalent, mixed numbers and improper), decimals (understanding, adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing), measurements (length, weight, volume, customary and metric, converting metric units), perimeter/area, lines, angles, quadrilaterals, other polygons, congruence and symmetry, bar models to solve multi-step word problems, money, time and time elapsed time.
Bible
Students study the book of Philippians, Judges, Joshua, King Saul, King David, King Solomon, Ruth, Samuel, the divided kingdom, the feast of trumpets, the day of atonement, and the fruit of the spirit.
Science
Students explore energy, speed, moving objects, waves, the earth’s features, natural hazards, structures, and functions.
Social Studies
Students study the state of North Carolina, including regions, history, government, and environmental and technological factors that affect the growth and development of the state.

## Fifth Grade

*(tile `post_id=672`, verbatim)*

Language Arts
Grammar: Parts of speech (nouns, adjectives, verbs, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions), suffixes/prefixes, analogies, contractions, compound words, singular/plural, subjects/predicates, identifying and writing complete sentences, punctuation/capitalization, prepositional phrases, clauses.
Reading Comprehension Predicting/inferring, visualizing/verbalizing, summarizing, sequencing, making connections, drawing conclusions, compare/contrast, identifying (main ideas, details cause/effect, author’s purpose, point of view, story elements, theme), genres (fiction, fantasy, biographies, history fiction, non-fiction, poetry), reading accuracy, fluency and expression, vocabulary and morphology.
Writing: Conventions (spelling, syllable division, writing mechanics), structure (identify audience, purpose, personal voice, writer’s viewpoint, organizing information, effective sentences - opening, closings combining, variety, elaboration - reasons, details, developing ideas, staying on topic, paragraphing - topic sentence, details, word choice - vivid, sensory details, description figurative language, writing process).Written expression includes descriptive, narrative, expository, persuasive writings, and literary analysis.
Math
Students master skills in place value to 10,000,000, mental math, estimation, multi-digit multiplication, division of numbers, fractions (adding and subtracting, equivalent, mixed numbers and improper), bar graph problem solving models, algebraic thinking, area/perimeter, ratios, decimals and percents, graphs, probability, types of angles, and number sense.
Bible
Students study the book of 1 Peter, Old Testament covenants, Judah’s return, writers of the Gospel and how the Gospels were written, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Jesus’ birth, early life and ministry, Hannukah, Jesus’ expanded ministry and growing popularity, Passover, parables, Jesus’ Galilean ministry, His death and resurrection, and His further ministry.
Science
Students discover chemical and physical property change, gravity, energy, photosynthesis and decomposition of plants and earth systems.
Social Studies
Students explore world and state geography, ancient Mesopotamia, ancient Greece and Rome, Vikings, Medieval Europe, Renaissance and the Reformation, Age of Exploration, Mayas, Incas, and Aztecs.

## Specials

*(tile `post_id=673`, verbatim)*

Art
Skill, craftsmanship, beauty, clarity, balance, and other timeless elements are studied and practiced in lower school art classes. Art education sets students on a trajectory for understanding the beauty of Christ and His creation. Students practice painting, sketching, ceramics, printmaking, landscapes, portraits, and many other projects. The annual spring Art Walk event allows students to display their work for family and friends. 
Music
The lower school music program exposes students to music in various ways. Vocal, instrumental, and percussion music is practiced while studying essential concepts like melody and rhythm through classical and modern composers. Fourth-grade students learn to play the recorder, while fifth-graders play band instruments. Vocal and instrumental concerts are held at Christmas and throughout the spring. 4th & 5th grade students interested in singing can join the children’s choir. In addition, private piano, vocal, and guitar lessons are available during school hours and after school.
CREATE Junior
CREATE Junior integrates technology and critical thinking skills with the content areas at each grade level. Students solve problems and build collaboration skills through science, technology, math, and engineering. CREATE Junior is a precursor to the CDS Middle School’s CREATE. 
Spanish
The lower school Spanish program teaches children to learn the language and also to engage in the culture. Holidays, geography, and traditions of Spanish communities are studied. 
Physical Education
Students flock to P.E. each week to practice various athletic skills and build endurance. The P.E. teacher encourages sportsmanship through encouragement and team building. Lessons center around developmentally appropriate skills. The Turkey Trot and Field Day are highlights for lower school students. 
Library
The lower school library encourages the joy of reading and exploration of various genres. The librarian instructs students on how to find books and wisely develop their research skills in print and online. Students participate in the Readers Are Leaders program, which encourages them to read for pleasure with a purpose. The library team chooses literature with great intentionality and care.
