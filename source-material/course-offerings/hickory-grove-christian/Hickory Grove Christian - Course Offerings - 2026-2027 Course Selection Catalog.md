## Certificate / diploma tracks

Three endorsement tracks the catalog documents (pages 8–10):

- **AP Capstone Diploma™** — scores of 3+ on AP Seminar, AP Research and four further AP
  exams. Scores of 3+ on Seminar and Research alone earn the **AP Seminar and Research
  Certificate™**.
- **Global Missions and Language Certificate** — all graduation requirements, three units
  of a foreign language, two units of honors Bible plus Honors Introduction to Philosophy,
  active church membership with a pastor's recommendation, at least one international
  mission trip, and a Missions Learning Practicum or missional student internship.
- **Medical Sciences Certificate** — Biology I (Honors or CP), Principles of Chemistry and
  Physics or Chemistry I Honors, Anatomy & Physiology I and II Honors, and Health Sciences
  (with AHA Basic Life Support certification), plus a clinical component: 24+ documented
  hours of approved medical internship, or licensure as a CNA. Students must notify
  administration by the start of sophomore year.

## AP eligibility policy (page 14, verbatim points)

- All students taking Advanced Placement courses are **required to take the AP exam**.
- For **AP Biology, Calculus and Chemistry**, a grade of **80 or higher** must be earned in
  the first-semester prerequisite course to proceed into the AP course.
- Students not meeting AP standards are placed on probation and risk the course being
  re-levelled from AP to honors on the transcript, or dropped.

### Advanced Placement (catalog pages 15–20)

#### Advanced Placement 2D Studio Art

- **Grade Level(s):** None
- **Prerequisite:** Students will need to have Art I-IV. Students who are missing one of these classes will need to conference with the art teacher for written approval. Portfolio samples may be required.
- **Course Description:** AP 2D Studio Art is centered around the creation of a 15-piece Sustained Investigation. A sustained investigation is a documented process of practice, experimentation, and revision that is driven by an essential question. Students will explore a topic of their choice, formulate the questions they seek to address, and create 15 artworks that express the development of their central ideas through their attempts to answer these questions. Throughout this investigation, students will be required to document their thought processes, compositional studies, practice and exploration with different media, and changes made to their finished pieces. The focus of the course will be on visual expression of ideas, as students begin to think purposefully about artistic decisions, and begin to make connections between their ideas, compositional choices, and use of media. In addition to the sustained investigation, students will also submit 5 selected works that represent their highest achievements in art. These 5 pieces may come from the sustained investigation, but are not required to. The final component of the AP portfolio will be a written artist’s statement, identifying the essential questions that guided the sustained investigation, and an explanation of the processes and ideas behind the work. As always, the highest purpose of this course is that the study of art will create more observant individuals who can appreciate the beauty of the natural world, with the hope that they will come to see God as an Artist and draw closer to Him through the process of making art.

#### Advanced Placement Biology

- **Grade Level(s):** 10-12
- **Prerequisite:** Honors Biology/Honors Biology II/Honors Chemistry or Honors Physical Science/Approval Required
- **Course Description:** Students taking AP Biology will be prepared for undergraduate exemption from a standard Introductory Biology class at most major colleges and universities commensurate with an approved score on the national AP exam. Topics include: Evolution and Diversity of Life, Cells and Molecules, Genetics, Molecular Biology, and Plant and Animal Structure and Function. There are AP/College Board approved laboratory exercises that are mandatory for course participation. The course is currently double-blocked to accommodate laboratory activities. Extensive laboratory experience and scientific methodology and reporting will be expected. Students should carefully consider their level of commitment to the course of study prior to enrollment. This class requires extensive independent reading and research writing. The students will be required to take the AP final exam in May in lieu of a semester final. This course is double blocked with Honors Biology II.

#### Advanced Placement Calculus AB

- **Grade Level(s):** 11-12
- **Prerequisite:** Honors Calculus/Approval Required
- **Course Description:** This one-credit course covers topics in both differential and integral calculus and is presented on a college level. The course is designed to prepare students for the AP Calculus exam as well as other college calculus classes. Topics include limits, differentiation, and integration as well as their applications. Honors Precalculus is a prerequi- site in order to ensure that students have a strong math background and strong understanding of the Unit Circle, but Calculus is a whole new course dealing with changing values and the rates of that change that are not a part of Precalculus. In college, Calculus is reserved for Engineering and Business majors so only students going into those fields are encouraged to take this class. The course stresses learning by problem solving and critical thinking and is designed for only the most serious high school math student. TI-83 or TI-84 calculators are required and used extensively throughout the course. In addition, subscription to WebAssign, an online homework program, is required. Students are required to take the AP Calculus exam at the completion of the course. Students should carefully consider their level of commitment to this course before enrolling. This course is double blocked with Honors Calculus.

#### Introduction to Ethics - Honors

- **Grade Level(s):** 12
- **Prerequisite:** Introduction to Philosophy, Approval Required
- **Course Description:** This course explores the foundations of ethical thought, beginning with classical philosophy and lead- ing to the development of a biblically grounded ethical framework for contemporary application. Students will engage with key thinkers such as Plato, Aristotle, Augustine, and Aquinas, whose insights into virtue, natural law, and moral reasoning have shaped ethical discourse throughout history. Building on the foundational concepts introduced in Introduction to Philosophy— metaphysics, epistemology, and axiology—this course deepens students’ understanding of axiology by examining the nature of moral values and the basis for ethical decision-making. The study of Christian ethics will be rooted in a biblical worldview, exploring how classical thought informs Christian moral reasoning and how ethical principles apply to contemporary issues. Throughout the course, students will analyze the role of human nature (anthropology) in ethical formation, evaluating compet- ing ethical systems and their implications for modern society. By integrating philosophical inquiry with scriptural foundations, students will be equipped to discern and apply ethical principles in personal, cultural, and professional contexts.

#### Introduction to Philosophy - Honors

- **Grade Level(s):** 11-12
- **Prerequisite:** Approval Required
- **Course Description:** Western Philosophy is rooted in a great tradition of seeking wisdom for the good life in three central categories: Metaphysics, Epistemology, and Axiology. In Introduction to Philosophy, students will be introduced to these concepts through the writings of several major philosophers and how their philosophies have left a lasting legacy in shaping Western thought and culture (s) for thousands of years. Building on these foundational ideas, students will develop an understanding of what a worldview is along with developing worldview discernment in reading key biblical passages and influential texts that highlight how the Christian worldview uniquely fulfills the deeper longing of Classical philosophy in the person of Jesus Christ. As the Word of God, the eternal Son bridges the gap of both the Hebrew and classical Greek mindsets in a way that no other person in history has done, offering a bridge between otherwise very different traditions. Students will take these principles and navigate how the Christian tradition and worldview provides answers to life’s deepest questions in ways that no other worldview can. Drawing from these ideas, students will be encouraged in their faith and challenged as to how these foundations of wisdom can mold them for a life of worldview confidence, faithful living, and influence for the glory of God and His Kingdom— the good life.
