# Charlotte Latin School — College Support — Redesign Research 2026

## Provenance

- **Who:** Researched by Claude (Opus 5) via live web research on behalf of the project owner.
- **When:** 2026-07-26.
- **How:** Primary extraction from Charlotte Latin School's own published materials — the
  **2025–26 Upper School Profile PDF** (served via the school's Finalsite resource manager),
  the College Counseling and College Acceptances pages, and school news posts. Historical
  comparison drawn from three archived Upper School Profile editions (2017–18, 2019–20,
  2020–21) still hosted on charlottelatin.org. Selectivity buckets scored against the
  **2026 U.S. News** Best Colleges rankings.
- **Method notes:** The 2025–26 Upper School Profile PDF was downloaded and its text
  extracted programmatically (pypdf). The 2023–25 college acceptance list was parsed
  mechanically from that PDF into 300 institution rows, and matriculation asterisks were
  preserved per-row. Selectivity bucket counts below were computed by matching that parsed
  list against 2026 U.S. News rank tables — they are **derived counts, not school-published
  figures**, and are tagged accordingly.
- **Key headline for the redesign:** Charlotte Latin is an **outlier in what it withholds**.
  The current profile publishes **no SAT scores, no ACT scores, no AP score distribution,
  no GPA distribution, and no class rank/quintile/decile table**. Earlier editions of the
  same document *did* publish AP and SAT/ACT results. Multiple cards below therefore carry
  large, honest "NOT PUBLISHED" blocks.

**Exam policy (published, unambiguous):** "Students in AP classes are **required to take the
AP exam**." Placement is gated: "Students must meet certain benchmarks to be placed by faculty
in Honors or AP courses, **with the exception of AP Psychology, AP Economics, AP Environmental

are faculty-gated.

### Post-AP / advanced course depth

The profile lists Honors courses that function as post-AP or parallel-advanced work:

- **Mathematics beyond the AP line:** Honors Data Analytics, Honors Intro to Probability &
  Statistics, Honors Math of Social Choice & Management Science. **No multivariable calculus
  or linear algebra is listed** — a genuine ceiling worth noting. The top listed math course
  remains AP Calculus BC.
- **Computer science beyond AP CS A:** **Honors Java Data Structures**, plus AP Cybersecurity 1.
- **Engineering (Honors, non-AP):** Honors Bioengineering, Honors Electrical Engineering.
- **World language beyond AP:** **Honors Greek I–IV** (Greek may only be taken as a *second*
  world language), Honors Latin III/IV, Honors French III/IV, Honors Spanish III/IV, plus both
  AP Spanish Language **and** AP Spanish Literature.
- **Interdisciplinary:** Honors American Studies-History (double-period seminar co-created by
  the History and English departments, taught "at an equivalent level of rigor and depth to
  Advanced Placement curricula" per the 2017–18 profile).
- **Distinctive history seminars:** Honors Food in World History, Honors History of Music
  Seminar, Honors Global Immigration and Migration, Honors Defining Moments: The Civil Rights
  Movement.

**Beyond-campus options:** Older profiles state that electives taken through **NCAIS VisNet**
(the North Carolina Association of Independent Schools virtual network) "are reported on the
CLS transcript but are **not included in the CLS GPA**." The **2025–26 profile does not mention
VisNet or any online/dual-enrollment option** — `TO VERIFY` whether this pathway still exists.

### How the grade is engineered to be trusted

| Mechanism | What Latin publishes |
|---|---|
| GPA scale | **Weighted 4-point scale.** "AP and Honors courses receive **one additional quality point**." (2017–18 profile; the 2025–26 profile omits this statement — see discrepancy below.) |
| Letter/quality-point table | 2017–18 profile published a full table: A+ 4.33 (98–100), A 4.00 (93–97), A− 3.67 (90–92), B+ 3.33 (87–89), B 3.00 (83–86), B− 2.67 (80–82), C+ 2.33 (77–79), C 2.00 (73–76), C− 1.67 (70–72), D+ 1.33 (67–69), D 1.00 (63–66), D− 0.67 (60–62), F 0.00 (<60) |
| Class rank | **"Charlotte Latin School does not report class rank."** (2017–18 profile) |
| Quintile / decile distribution | — **NOT PUBLISHED** in any edition reviewed |
| GPA distribution | — **NOT PUBLISHED** |
| Middle School credit handling | "High school classes completed in Middle School are indicated on the transcript but are **not included in any GPA**." (2025–26 profile) |
| "Most rigorous" course-load norm | Defined structurally, not by label: six courses required per year, four must be academic; **"the recommended and typical course load is five academic courses and one enrichment class."** 21 credits to graduate (English 4, Math 4, Science 3, History 3 incl. World History I & II and U.S. History, World Language 3 in the same language, PE 1 with ½ waivable for athletics). |

**Math-placement transparency (unusually candid, and a real signal to admissions readers):**
the profile explicitly warns colleges not to misread standard-track placement —

> "Our non-honors math curriculum consists of Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Advanced
> Functions/Trig (AFT), Precalculus, and Calculus. In general, students may only 'skip' AFT by
> earning an 'A' or 'A−' in Algebra II. Precalculus does not lead directly to AP Calculus; the
> AP Calculus curriculum begins at the end of Honors Precalculus. As our Honors math curriculum
> is unusually rigorous and fast-paced, **many fine math students are placed in 'standard' courses**."

> **Discrepancy flagged — GPA/rank policy disappeared from the current profile.** The
> 2017–18 profile carried an explicit "MARKING SYSTEM" block (weighted 4-point scale, one
> extra quality point for AP/Honors, no class rank) **and** a full letter-grade/quality-point
> table. The **2025–26 profile contains none of this** — no grading scale, no weighting
> statement, no class-rank policy. A college reader of the current profile cannot tell how
> Latin weights or whether it ranks. Both states are documented above; treat the weighting
> and no-rank policy as **`TO VERIFY` for 2025–26** since only the older edition attests them.

> **Publishing-quality defect (worth a tour question).** The published 2025–26 Upper School
> Profile PDF contains an **unedited internal editorial comment left in the live document**,
> immediately after the Academic Program section: *"We'll need to somehow check to make sure
> all these students are still here, yes?"* This is verbatim in the file currently served from
> the school's own resource manager as of 2026-07-26. It is a proofreading lapse in the single
> document every admissions office reads.

### Four-year 9→12 timeline (verbatim programming)

**Every year (all grades):**
- College information available on **MyLatin and SCOIR**
- Seminars on various college admission topics, such as extracurriculars and summer enrichment
- Periodic informational emails and postings
- Regular appointments and drop-ins
- Informative programs on **Financial Aid for College, Collegiate Athletics, and Arts in College**

**Grade 9 (Freshman):**
- A series of seminars to help students make the most of their Upper School experience
- **SCOIR registration**
- FAQ meeting for parents

**Grade 10 (Sophomore):**
- Seminars on self-discovery — interests and how they translate into college choices
- Individual meetings with counselor on course selection and registration
- **Case Studies Mock College Admission Exercise**, followed by a **College Fair**
- FAQ meeting for parents

**Grade 11 (Junior):**
- Seminars on college visits, interviews, and building an activity résumé
- **Optional summer essay workshops**
- College representative visits
- Individual meetings with counselors on course selection and registration
- **College admissions event featuring a Dean's Panel**
- **Joint meeting** amongst counselor, parent/guardian, and student on the student's
  post-secondary journey
- College Counseling event for parents
- Discussion of **SAT and ACT preparation options and testing timeline**

**Grade 12 (Senior):**
- **College Application Bootcamp**
- Essay review and feedback
- Seminars reviewing the application process
- College rep visits
- **Application assistance, including proofreading each essay and application prior to submission**
- Grade 12 Parent College Night
- **Transition to College Program** for parents

### Application mechanics the office owns

- **Applications:** "review every application prior to submission" (2017–18 profile);
  current page: "Application assistance, including **proofreading each essay and application
  prior to submission**."
- **Essays:** three dedicated essay specialists; senior essay review and feedback; optional
  junior summer essay workshops.
- **Testing:** counselors work with students "on standardized testing timelines"; junior-year
  discussion of SAT/ACT prep options and timeline.
- **Interviews:** "strategies for effective interviews"; the 2017–18 profile specifies
  **mock interviews**.
- **Financial aid:** recurring "Financial Aid for College" program (all-years track).
- **Recruiting/arts tracks:** recurring "Collegiate Athletics" and "Arts in College" programs.
- **Teacher recommendations:** — **NOT PUBLISHED** as an office-owned process. No description
  of how teacher recs are solicited, assigned, or capped.
- **Scholarship search:** — **NOT PUBLISHED** as a named service. No scholarship-search
  platform or dedicated scholarship advising is described on the current page.
