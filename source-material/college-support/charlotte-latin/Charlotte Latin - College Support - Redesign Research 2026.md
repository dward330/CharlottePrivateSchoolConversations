# Charlotte Latin School — College Support — Redesign Research 2026

## Provenance

- **Who:** Researched by Claude (Opus 5) via live web research on behalf of the project owner.
- **When:** 2026-07-26.
- **How:** Primary extraction from Charlotte Latin School's own published materials — the
  **2025–26 Upper School Profile PDF** (served via the school's Finalsite resource manager),
  the College Counseling and College Acceptances pages, and school news posts. Historical
  comparison drawn from three archived Upper School Profile editions (2017–18, 2019–20,
  2020–21) still hosted on charlottelatin.org. Selectivity buckets scored against the
  **2026 U.S. News** Best Colleges rankings.
- **Method notes:** The 2025–26 Upper School Profile PDF was downloaded and its text
  extracted programmatically (pypdf). The 2023–25 college acceptance list was parsed
  mechanically from that PDF into 300 institution rows, and matriculation asterisks were
  preserved per-row. Selectivity bucket counts below were computed by matching that parsed
  list against 2026 U.S. News rank tables — they are **derived counts, not school-published
  figures**, and are tagged accordingly.
- **Key headline for the redesign:** Charlotte Latin is an **outlier in what it withholds**.
  The current profile publishes **no SAT scores, no ACT scores, no AP score distribution,
  no GPA distribution, and no class rank/quintile/decile table**. Earlier editions of the
  same document *did* publish AP and SAT/ACT results. Multiple cards below therefore carry
  large, honest "NOT PUBLISHED" blocks.

### How the grade is engineered to be trusted

| Mechanism | What Latin publishes |
|---|---|
| GPA scale | **Weighted 4-point scale.** "AP and Honors courses receive **one additional quality point**." (2017–18 profile; the 2025–26 profile omits this statement — see discrepancy below.) |
| Letter/quality-point table | 2017–18 profile published a full table: A+ 4.33 (98–100), A 4.00 (93–97), A− 3.67 (90–92), B+ 3.33 (87–89), B 3.00 (83–86), B− 2.67 (80–82), C+ 2.33 (77–79), C 2.00 (73–76), C− 1.67 (70–72), D+ 1.33 (67–69), D 1.00 (63–66), D− 0.67 (60–62), F 0.00 (<60) |
| Class rank | **"Charlotte Latin School does not report class rank."** (2017–18 profile) |
| Quintile / decile distribution | — **NOT PUBLISHED** in any edition reviewed |
| GPA distribution | — **NOT PUBLISHED** |
| Middle School credit handling | "High school classes completed in Middle School are indicated on the transcript but are **not included in any GPA**." (2025–26 profile) |
| "Most rigorous" course-load norm | Defined structurally, not by label: six courses required per year, four must be academic; **"the recommended and typical course load is five academic courses and one enrichment class."** 21 credits to graduate (English 4, Math 4, Science 3, History 3 incl. World History I & II and U.S. History, World Language 3 in the same language, PE 1 with ½ waivable for athletics). |

**Math-placement transparency (unusually candid, and a real signal to admissions readers):**
the profile explicitly warns colleges not to misread standard-track placement —

> "Our non-honors math curriculum consists of Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Advanced
> Functions/Trig (AFT), Precalculus, and Calculus. In general, students may only 'skip' AFT by
> earning an 'A' or 'A−' in Algebra II. Precalculus does not lead directly to AP Calculus; the
> AP Calculus curriculum begins at the end of Honors Precalculus. As our Honors math curriculum
> is unusually rigorous and fast-paced, **many fine math students are placed in 'standard' courses**."

> **Discrepancy flagged — GPA/rank policy disappeared from the current profile.** The
> 2017–18 profile carried an explicit "MARKING SYSTEM" block (weighted 4-point scale, one
> extra quality point for AP/Honors, no class rank) **and** a full letter-grade/quality-point
> table. The **2025–26 profile contains none of this** — no grading scale, no weighting
> statement, no class-rank policy. A college reader of the current profile cannot tell how
> Latin weights or whether it ranks. Both states are documented above; treat the weighting
> and no-rank policy as **`TO VERIFY` for 2025–26** since only the older edition attests them.

> **Publishing-quality defect (worth a tour question).** The published 2025–26 Upper School
> Profile PDF contains an **unedited internal editorial comment left in the live document**,
> immediately after the Academic Program section: *"We'll need to somehow check to make sure
> all these students are still here, yes?"* This is verbatim in the file currently served from
> the school's own resource manager as of 2026-07-26. It is a proofreading lapse in the single
> document every admissions office reads.

### Four-year 9→12 timeline (verbatim programming)

**Every year (all grades):**
- College information available on **MyLatin and SCOIR**
- Seminars on various college admission topics, such as extracurriculars and summer enrichment
- Periodic informational emails and postings
- Regular appointments and drop-ins
- Informative programs on **Financial Aid for College, Collegiate Athletics, and Arts in College**

**Grade 9 (Freshman):**
- A series of seminars to help students make the most of their Upper School experience
- **SCOIR registration**
- FAQ meeting for parents

**Grade 10 (Sophomore):**
- Seminars on self-discovery — interests and how they translate into college choices
- Individual meetings with counselor on course selection and registration
- **Case Studies Mock College Admission Exercise**, followed by a **College Fair**
- FAQ meeting for parents

**Grade 11 (Junior):**
- Seminars on college visits, interviews, and building an activity résumé
- **Optional summer essay workshops**
- College representative visits
- Individual meetings with counselors on course selection and registration
- **College admissions event featuring a Dean's Panel**
- **Joint meeting** amongst counselor, parent/guardian, and student on the student's
  post-secondary journey
- College Counseling event for parents
- Discussion of **SAT and ACT preparation options and testing timeline**

**Grade 12 (Senior):**
- **College Application Bootcamp**
- Essay review and feedback
- Seminars reviewing the application process
- College rep visits
- **Application assistance, including proofreading each essay and application prior to submission**
- Grade 12 Parent College Night
- **Transition to College Program** for parents

### The honest acceptance-vs-matriculation caveat

**This is the single most important interpretive warning attached to this card.**

- The 300-name list is an **acceptance** list aggregated over **three graduating classes**, not a
  matriculation list and not one year. With roughly 130–145 seniors per class, the three classes
  together total **roughly 400 graduates** producing **300 distinct acceptance destinations**.
- **169 of the 300 institutions (56%) carry no matriculation asterisk at all** — meaning that for
  a clear majority of names on the list, **no Latin student is recorded as having enrolled** during
  2023–25. A family scanning the list for a specific college is, more often than not, looking at a
  place where a Latin student was *admitted* but did *not* go.
- Only **131 institutions (44%)** have at least one confirmed matriculant across all three classes.
- The asterisk carries **no magnitude**. "Harvard University*" and "Wingate University*" are
  typographically identical; one matriculant over three years earns the same mark as thirty.
- **A single acceptance anywhere on the list puts that college's name in print for three years.**
  Ivy and Ivy Plus names in particular may rest on **one** admitted student across ~400 graduates.
- Latin publishes **no per-college counts, no matriculation totals, no "most attended" table, and
  no class-by-class breakdown** — so the list cannot be converted into a placement rate for any
  institution. Any such rate would be **invented**, and none appears in this file.

#### 1f. The honest summary of Lever 1

**There is no required capstone, no required senior project, no required internship, and no

can graduate from Latin with a strong transcript and no distinguishing hook whatsoever.

## Card — Verdict & Visit Checklist

### Why it holds up

**1. The counseling office is deep, specialized, and unusually stable.** Four full-time counselors
plus a registrar, an administrative assistant and **three dedicated essay specialists** (nine
staff) for **145 seniors**. Three of the four counselors — Jennings, Smith, Cripps — are documented
in the same office as far back as the **2017–18** profile, giving the director **9+ years of
verifiable tenure**. Essay specialists are rare in peer offices, and the office commits to
"**proofreading each essay and application prior to submission**." *(Card 2)*

**2. Two genuinely elite, nationally-validated spikes.** Speech & Debate holds the **NSDA Debate
School of Excellence Award** — a **top-20 program nationally**, with a **5th-place Public Forum
finish at NSDA Nationals** and a two-Diamond director. The **Fab Lab is an accredited Fab Academy
node in MIT's Center for Bits and Atoms network**, operating since 2016 with 50 node graduates.
Both are third-party validated, not self-declared. *(Card 4)*

**3. The acceptance list demonstrates real range, top to bottom.** Across the Classes of 2023–25,
**300 institutions** with **131 carrying matriculation asterisks**: five Ivies (all matriculated),
Duke and MIT, both service academies, **63 of 64 Power Four schools**, 11 international
universities — alongside a deep regional ladder where **every UNC-system campus shows a
matriculant**. Latin visibly serves the middle of its class, not only the top. *(Card 3)*

**4. Academic rigor is structurally real and honestly framed.** **23 AP subjects**, AP exams
**required** of AP students, faculty-gated placement in all but four APs, and a 418-award AP Scholar
ledger across 2023–25. The profile's candid warning that "**many fine math students are placed in
'standard' courses**" is the kind of statement that builds credibility with admissions readers.
*(Card 1)*

**5. Recognition and merit outcomes are documented with named students.** **12 National Merit
Finalists and 18 Commended** in the Class of 2026 with **five National Merit Scholarship winners
named**; a full-ride **Johnson Scholarship at Washington & Lee** (Kelsey Sciacca '26); named
service-academy appointments; a **$154,000+ National ROTC Scholarship**. The Class of 2023 reported
**231 merit scholarships totaling more than $13 million**, with **48 students offered over $100,000
each**. *(Cards 1 and 3)*

**6. Institutional standing is long-established.** North Carolina's **only Malone Scholars School**;
the **youngest school in the nation** ever awarded a Cum Laude Society chapter; SAIS-accredited;
and — per archived profiles — a broad integrity-reporting policy complying with the **NACAC
Statement of Principles of Good Practice**. *(Card 4)*

### Ask on the tour

These target precisely what Latin does **not** publish. Each is answerable, and each closes a
documented gap in this file.

**1. Testing and AP outcomes — why did they disappear, and what are they now?**
"Your 2017–18 profile published SAT means (670 EBRW / 680 Math), an ACT composite mean of 29.1, and
2017 AP results (87.3% scoring 3+). **No SAT, ACT, or AP score data has appeared since.** What are
the current class's figures, and what is the reasoning for not publishing them? Will you share them
with families on request?"

**2. GPA distribution and class rank — what does the current profile actually tell colleges?**
"The 2017–18 profile stated a weighted 4-point scale, one extra quality point for AP/Honors, and
'**Charlotte Latin School does not report class rank.**' **None of those statements appear in the
2025–26 profile** — no grading scale, no weighting, no rank policy. Do you still weight? Do you
still decline to rank? **How does a college reader calibrate a Latin transcript today?** And is
there any GPA distribution or quintile data you share with colleges but not publicly?"

**3. Learning Resources in the Upper School — who staffs it, and is it still there?**
"Every Learning Resources page on your site now **404s**, and the tile on the Student Support and
Wellness page **links nowhere**. The archived guidelines describe the Upper School program as
'**consultative and coaching**' rather than remedial, with sessions '**billed per session each
month**' outside tuition. **How many LR teachers serve grades 9–12? How many students do they
serve? Who is the current Director? What does an Upper School session cost? And how do you support
a student who needs direct remediation, not coaching?**"

**4. The profile's publishing defects — including the comment left in the live document.**
"The published 2025–26 Upper School Profile contains an **unremoved internal editorial comment** —
'*We'll need to somehow check to make sure all these students are still here, yes?*' — in the one
document every admissions office reads. It also **no longer carries your CEEB code (340663), your
professional memberships (NACAC, SACAC, NAIS, SAIS, NCAIS, ACCIS), or your discipline-reporting and
NACAC Principles of Good Practice statement**, all of which appeared in earlier editions. **Are
those memberships and that reporting policy still in force, and when will the profile be corrected?**"

**5. Acceptances versus matriculation — what do the numbers behind the list look like?**
"The 2023–25 list covers **three graduating classes** and **169 of its 300 institutions carry no
matriculation asterisk**. **How many students actually enrolled at each of your most-cited
colleges? What percentage of the most recent class enrolled at a four-year college** — the figure
your profile published as 100% through 2021 but dropped thereafter — **and what were the total

**6. The spike problem — what exists for a student who isn't in Debate or the Fab Lab?**
"There is **no required capstone, senior project, independent study, or school-wide internship**,
and Global Studies — with seven sister schools — **confers no certificate or diploma**. The

credit, and how many students take it?"

**7. Counseling load and non-traditional paths.**
"With four counselors and students assigned from freshman year, the all-grades ratio is roughly
