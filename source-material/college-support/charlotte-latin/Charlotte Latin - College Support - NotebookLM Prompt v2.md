============================ PASTE FROM HERE ============================

Create a warm two-host episode, "Inside Charlotte Latin College Support," for parents, on how Charlotte Latin School (independent K-12, Charlotte NC) prepares and places students for college. Cover ONLY Charlotte Latin; no comparisons.

GLOBAL RULES (whole episode):
- No inflated words (elite/powerhouse/world-class/dynasty) unless a fact supports it.
- "Unpublished is not deficient": items the school doesn't publish are transparency gaps, NOT proof of weakness. Say so each time one comes up.
- At most ONE analogy all episode; no cold-open metaphor.
- Never say a student's name; aggregate only.
- Bracketed [notes] are for you; never read aloud.
- All seven segments PLUS a distinct closing beat are required; if short, cut early chatter, not a later segment. Segments 5 and 7 drop most often, so give EACH its own beat with 2+ specifics.

Open by stating the agenda plainly: counseling team, academics, leverage, application help, standing out, outcomes, the honest fit questions.

(1) COUNSELING ENGINE. Four full-time counselors plus a registrar, an admin, and three essay specialists; each student gets a counselor freshman year (~36 seniors per counselor at peak); the core team is long-stable. SCOIR (say "score"; never spell S-C-O-I-R) shows scattergrams of past applicants' GPA and scores versus real admit/deny results. Note plainly: none of the four is a former admissions officer, a fact, not a knock.

(2) ACADEMIC CASE. A.P. in 23 subjects, and every A.P. student takes the exam; the current profile reports 193 A.P. Scholars with Distinction over three years, and 12 National Merit Semifinalists in the Class of 2026. Grading is weighted four-point with an extra point for Honors and A.P.; no class rank. Gap: no published A.P. pass rates (share scoring 3+) or current SAT/ACT ranges; review-site scores are self-reported, not the school's.

(3) INSTITUTIONAL LEVERAGE. The School Profile is what colleges read with the transcript. Two lead credentials: North Carolina's only Malone Scholars School (nationally capped) and a Cum Laude Society chapter since 1976. Colleges visit via Rep Visits; sophomores run a mock-admissions "Case Studies" exercise plus a College Fair. Don't claim secret admissions-office relationships; none are published.

(4) APPLICATION SUPPORT. Standout: three essay specialists; every application reviewed before submission. Keep two money topics separate: (a) college merit, where students have won the Morehead-Cain and Robertson at U.N.C.-Chapel Hill, though class-wide dollars aren't published; and (b) the school's OWN tuition aid, over three million dollars via a platform, Clarity, the affordability question, not what colleges award. Need-based navigation (FAF-suh, the C.S.S. Profile) in seminars.

(5) STANDING OUT [required, 2+ specifics]. A distinct "hook": sophomore self-discovery seminars turn interests into direction; activity-resume seminars make a profile coherent. Specialized paths: a Collegiate Athletics seminar (recruiting support) and an Arts in College seminar (portfolios, auditions). These are process supports, not a guaranteed "spike."

(6) OUTCOMES. The published "Acceptances by the Classes of 2023 to 2025" is an acceptance list of ~299 colleges; an asterisk marks where at least one student matriculated (matriculation means where students actually enroll). Report BREADTH, not a placement rate: of the eight Ivies, five appear (Brown, Cornell, Dartmouth, Harvard, Yale). By "Ivy Plus" (the eight Ivies plus peers like M.I.T., Stanford, Duke, and Chicago, seventeen in all), twelve of seventeen appear. Roughly sixty-five Top Seventy-Five national universities, forty Top Seventy-Five liberal-arts, and sixty Power Four schools appear. Gaps: no per-class percentages, no yield (the share of admitted students who enroll), and no graduation or persistence data, all transparency gaps.

(7) FIT, RANK AND THE WHOLE CLASS [required, 2+ specifics]. No class rank, so strong students aren't pitted against each other. Its stance is fit over prestige: in its words, "Division One or Division Three, what matters most are the dreams students carry." Learning differences get a Learning Resources office plus an academic-transition office, and the range of destinations (arts schools, international universities, service academies) shows support when a student changes direction.

CLOSING - its own beat; do NOT end on Segment 7's content. After Segment 7, pivot ("To pull this together...") and give three one-line takeaways. Then, as the LITERAL FINAL SENTENCE (nothing after it), deliver the disclaimer in full, both halves: "We've reported only what Charlotte Latin publishes and what our research surfaced; where a figure isn't published, that's a transparency gap, not a verdict on the school's quality."

Pronunciation: SCOIR = "score" (never spell S-C-O-I-R); "A.P."; Division One/Two/Three; Power Four; Top Seventy-Five; Ivy Plus; FAFSA = "FAF-suh"; C.S.S. Profile; Early Decision/Early Action.
