### Global caveat on this school

Davidson Day is the **smallest school** in this comparison set — total enrollment 541, Upper
School 198, Class of 2026 = 47, Class of 2025 = 53. It publishes **considerably less** than the
larger Charlotte independents. Most notably it **deliberately withholds all SAT/ACT data**, and
it publishes **no AP exam performance data** and **no National Merit data whatsoever**.
Card 5 (Whole Class Analytics) is therefore **almost entirely empty of quantitative content** —
that is an honest finding, not a research failure. Nothing in this file has been estimated,
interpolated, or carried over from another school.

### Name-collision warning (critical)

**Davidson Day School (Davidson, NC)** must never be confused with **Davidson Academy
(Reno, Nevada / Davidson Academy Online)**, a school for profoundly gifted students that
publishes large National Merit Semifinalist cohorts (e.g. 30 semifinalists in the 2026 program).
Those figures belong to a **different institution** and must never be attributed to Davidson Day.
Searches for "Davidson" + "National Merit" return the Nevada school almost exclusively.

## Card — The Transcript Colleges See

### Curriculum model: AP, not IB

Davidson Day runs an **Advanced Placement** model. **There is no IB programme** and no other
alternative diploma track (no Cambridge/AICE, no dual-enrollment-as-core model). The School
Profile states the school "challenge[s] students with Advanced Placement (AP) courses in
English, Math, Science, History, World Language, Technology and the Arts."

The school's *own* alternative-credential layer is not a curriculum track but a set of three
**Diploma Distinctions** (Global Studies, STEMM, Business & Civic Engagement) layered on top of
the AP curriculum — see Card 4.

- **Math:** **Calculus III (Post AP)** — explicitly weighted +1.0, the same as an AP. This is
  the school's only named post-AP course.

  mile away — **NOT PUBLISHED**, and a notable absence worth asking about.

### How the grade is engineered to be trusted

This is where Davidson Day actually *is* transparent, and it is the strongest part of this card.

| D− | 60–62 | 0.67 |
| F | 59 and below | — |

**Weighting:**

- **Honors courses: +0.5**
- **Advanced Placement courses: +1.0**
- **Calculus III (post-AP): +1.0** — explicitly called out as weighted like an AP.

**What is excluded from the GPA** (published, and unusually explicit):

- Transfer courses
- Upper School courses taken during Middle School
- Independent Studies
- **Any course taken online**
- GPAs "reflect only courses taken at Davidson Day School"
- Transfer students' GPAs are excluded from the published class distribution
- If a student enters with outside credits, "a transcript from the previous school will be
  enclosed with the transcript sent to colleges"

**Class-rank policy: "Davidson Day does not rank its students."** — stated flatly in the Profile.

**In place of rank, the Profile publishes a three-point GPA distribution** for the Class of 2026:

| Highest | Median | Lowest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **4.60** | **3.96** | **2.49** |

*(GPAs for transfer students are not included in the distribution.)*

**There is NO quintile table and NO decile table** — **NOT PUBLISHED**. Three summary points is
the entire distribution disclosure. This is materially less than a quintile table gives an
admissions reader.

**"Most rigorous course load" norms: NOT PUBLISHED.** The Profile does not define what
constitutes the most demanding program, does not state a maximum number of APs a student may
take, and does not publish how the school answers the Common App counselor form's
curriculum-rigor question. There is no published AP-access gate or departmental approval policy.

**Minimum graduation requirements: 22.5 credits**

- English: 4.5 credits (incl. 0.5 in Composition)
- Math: 4 credits, minimum sequence Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, and 1 credit beyond Algebra II
- Science: 3 credits of lab science (Physics, Chemistry, Biology)
- History: 3 credits
- World Language: 3 credits **in the same language**
- Fine Arts: 1 credit
- PE and Health: 1 credit
- Electives: 3 credits
- Additionally: 0.5 credit **per season** may be earned for school athletics or approved
  off-campus fitness activity

**Honor Code (published verbatim):** "I will not lie, cheat, steal, or plagiarize, nor will I
tolerate the dishonorable behavior of others."

### Four-year 9→12 timeline

Davidson Day states that "the college search process begins from the moment they join our
community," with **individualized, student-centered counseling beginning in 9th grade** — not
junior year. That early start is the office's central design claim.

**Published event slate** (the office runs a predictable annual calendar):

| Season | Named events |
| --- | --- |
| **Fall** | Senior Night · Junior Planning Night · College 101 · **Senior Application Boot Camp** |
| **Spring** | Financial Aid Night · **Athletic Recruitment Workshop** · **Case Studies Program & College Fair** · Freshman & Sophomore Planning Night |

`TO VERIFY` — **this slate is not currently presented as a grade-by-grade table on the live
College Counseling page.** The page as fetched on 2026-07-26 does **not** carry a 9/10/11/12
breakdown; the event list above is corroborated by search-indexed davidsonday.org content and
by the in-repo dossier. **A true grade-by-grade curriculum map (what happens in 9th vs. 10th vs.
11th vs. 12th, month by month) is NOT PUBLISHED.** Only "begins in 9th grade" plus the
fall/spring event list is public. Note the event names map loosely to grades — Freshman &
Sophomore Planning Night (9–10), Junior Planning Night (11), Senior Night and Senior Application
Boot Camp (12) — but the school does not present it that way.

### Application mechanics the office owns

Per the school's description, the College Counseling office assists in **all aspects of the
college planning process**, specifically:

- Research and **creating a balanced college list**
- **Planning for standardized tests**
- **Application essay writing**
- **The financial aid process**
- **Career planning**
- Factors to weigh in making the final enrollment decision

Applications and materials are submitted through **Scoir**.

**NOT PUBLISHED / unconfirmed:**

- **Whether every application is reviewed before submission** — no such commitment is published.
- **Teacher recommendation management protocol** beyond Scoir submission — no published policy
  on how many recs, how teachers are approached, or rec-writing training.
- **Early Decision / Early Action doctrine** — no published stance on whether the office steers
  toward or away from binding ED.
- **FAFSA / CSS Profile navigation content** — Financial Aid Night exists as an annual event,
  but no detailed college-aid navigation material is published. **Caution:** Davidson Day uses a
  tool called **Clarity** for its **own tuition-assistance** process. That is *school* admission
  aid, **not** college FAFSA/CSS help — do not conflate the two.
- **Outcome scattergrams** — Scoir can support them; the school does not document providing them.
- **Essay-review turnaround, # of required meetings per year, or a counselor-letter process.**

### The acceptance-vs-matriculation caveat — READ THIS FIRST

The big list below is titled, on the Profile itself,
**"Colleges Accepting Davidson Day Students | 2021 – 2025."**

- It is a **FIVE-YEAR CUMULATIVE ACCEPTANCE list (2021–2025)**, not a single-year list and
  **not a matriculation list**.
- A college appears if it accepted **at least one** Davidson Day student **at any point across
  five graduating classes**.
- It is **not** a placement rate, **not** a count of students, and **not** evidence that anyone
  enrolled there.
- **Nothing on the list is bolded or otherwise marked to indicate enrollment.** The school does
  **not** publish which colleges students actually attended — **NOT PUBLISHED**. Contrast with
  the single-year figure that graduates "selected 35 different colleges" in 2025, where the
  35 names themselves are **not published**.

## Card — Verdict & Visit Checklist

### Why it holds up — evidence-backed verdict points

**1. The spike-building machinery is real, credited, and disproportionate to the school's size.**
AFAR is not a service trip. Davidson Day students excavate under permit from the Belize Institute
of Archaeology in conjunction with the BVAR project, at Cahal Pech — a site significant enough to
carry an Archaeological Institute of America interactive dig. The program's history appears in
**peer-reviewed literature** (*Heritage*, 2020). Fieldwork spans **four countries** with
documented start years (Belize 2009, Spain 2014, Greece 2017, Portugal 2018), students publish
and present at conferences, and **over a quarter of the student body participates in
international research**. For a 198-student Upper School, that is a genuinely uncommon asset,
and it produces exactly the kind of narrative that a test-optional file needs.

**2. The Global Studies Diploma sends a SECOND transcript and profile to colleges.**
This is structurally different from a "concentration" or an honors cord. Per the School Profile:
"An additional transcript and profile will be sent for students who are receiving this diploma."
That is an extra document in the admissions file, backed by four years of one language, a minimum
two weeks of international field research, and a capstone paper. The STEMM and Business tracks
add a **100-hour practicum**, quarterly Ethics Labs, an 11th-grade Research Methods Honors course,
a 12th-grade Capstone Research Honors mentorship, and a public **Scholars Research Conference**.

**3. The caseload is genuinely small, and the office starts in 9th grade.**
Two counselors for 198 Upper School students works out to roughly **1:99**, and about **1:24 at
the senior level** (47 seniors). Against public-school norms in the several hundreds, that makes
the school's "individualized, student-centered" language credible rather than promotional. The
start point is **9th grade, not junior year** — stated by the school.

**4. The School Profile does the work that test scores and rank would otherwise do.**
Given that Davidson Day publishes no test data and does not rank, the profile has to carry the
context load — and it does. Full grading scale with quality points, explicit +0.5/+1.0 weighting,
a stated list of what is excluded from the GPA, the Class of 2026 GPA range and median, the
complete course catalog by level with courses *not* offered this year clearly flagged, graduation
requirements, the Honor Code, and a five-year acceptance list. The flagging of unoffered courses
in particular is a small honesty signal most schools skip.

**5. The rep-visit volume and the Cannon School case-studies co-host are real access.**
"More than 80" college reps a year at a school with 198 Upper Schoolers is a high per-student
exposure rate, with visits running Monday–Friday all fall and students getting direct
conversation time. The spring **Case Studies Program and College Fair, co-hosted with Cannon
School**, puts families inside a mock admissions committee reading real (fictionalized) files —
and its fair drew **nearly 70 colleges**.

**6. The published outcomes are strong for the class size, and the low end of the GPA range is
disclosed.** Class of 2025: **53 graduates, 258 acceptances, 100% to four-year institutions,
$7.4M+ in merit offered, 11 NCAA athletes.** Separately, publishing a **lowest GPA of 2.49**
alongside a 4.60 high is a transparency choice many peer schools avoid — it signals the no-rank
policy is about protecting students, not about hiding the bottom of the class.

**The honest counterweight:** every one of the above is about *process, program, and access*.
Davidson Day publishes **no AP exam results, no SAT/ACT data, no National Merit standing, no
GPA quintiles, and no matriculation-by-college list**. A family that evaluates schools on
published outcome metrics will find less here than at any other school in this set. That is a
disclosure posture, not a demonstrated weakness — but it does mean the burden of verification
falls on the tour.

### Ask on the tour — questions targeting exactly what is NOT published

**1. "You don't publish AP exam results. What percentage of your AP exams scored 3 or higher last
year, and what share were 4s and 5s? Also — do you require students in an AP course to sit the
exam?"**
*Why:* the Profile lists a 21-course AP catalog but zero performance data. Offerings tell you
what a student *can* take; they say nothing about outcomes. The exam-policy question matters just
as much — a school that doesn't require the exam can look strong on enrollment while few students
test.

**2. "The Profile says you won't provide testing profiles because of test-optional policies. What
were the actual middle-50% SAT and ACT ranges for the last graduating class, and how many students
tested?"**
*Why:* this is the single largest gap. Test-optional is a legitimate reason to withhold *publicly*,
but the office knows the numbers, and the number of *testers* is as revealing as the scores — it
tells you whether the class is testing at all. Ask for both.

**3. "Has Davidson Day had any National Merit Semifinalists, Finalists, or Commended Students in
the last five years? How many per class?"**
*Why:* nothing is published and no public record exists. Ask directly rather than assume either
way. Be explicit that you mean **Davidson Day School in NC** — the name collides with Davidson
Academy in Nevada, which posts very large National Merit cohorts that are a different school's.

**4. "You publish a highest, median and lowest GPA — but no quintiles. What does the GPA
distribution actually look like? How many of the 47 seniors are above a 4.0, and where does the
25th percentile fall?"**
*Why:* three points give the range and midpoint but hide the shape. In a 47-person class, the
difference between a tightly clustered distribution and a barbell is the whole story for a family
trying to judge where their child would land.

**5. "Which 35 colleges did the Class of 2025 actually enroll at, and which colleges take a
Davidson Day student most years?"**
*Why:* the big list is a **five-year cumulative acceptance** list where one acceptance across five
classes earns a place — it is breadth, not placement. The school reports "35 different colleges
selected" but never names them. The matriculation list and any repeat pipeline are the numbers
that actually predict outcomes, and neither is published.

**6. "Does someone in the office read every application before it's submitted? What's your stance
on binding Early Decision, and how do you handle teacher recommendations?"**
*Why:* none of this is published. A two-person office *can* review everything — that's the
advantage of the small caseload — but Davidson Day never commits to it in writing. Get the answer
out loud.

**7. "Davidson College is one mile away. Is there any dual-enrollment, course-access, or
partnership arrangement with them — or with UNC Charlotte?"**
*Why:* the Profile itself flags the one-mile proximity to a highly selective liberal arts college,
yet no partnership, dual-enrollment, or course-access agreement is published anywhere. Post-AP
depth tops out at Calculus III, and Independent Studies don't even count toward the GPA. If that
door is open, it's a significant unadvertised asset; if it's closed, that's worth knowing too.

**8. "If my child has accommodations through the Learning Enrichment Center, who handles getting
those approved for College Board and ACT testing, and documented for college disability
services?"**
*Why:* the LEC is well documented as a support structure through grade 12, but its hand-off into
the college-application and college-testing process is documented nowhere. Ask the LEC *and* the
counseling office — the gap is usually in the seam between them.

## Discrepancies flagged (both sides shown, nothing silently resolved)

**1. College rep visits: "more than 80" vs. ~71 documented.**
The College Counseling page advertises welcoming **"more than 80 college admission
representatives from across the globe to campus each year"** — the school's standing benchmark,
still live on the page as of 2026-07-26. The in-repo dossier reports a documented **~71
representatives in a recent fall, with nearly 80% from out-of-state institutions**. I could
**not re-verify the 71 figure** from a live primary source in this pass — treat it as
`TO VERIFY`. Both should be shown: **"80+" is the marketing benchmark; ~71 is the reported
recent actual.** Do not average them.

**2. Student–faculty ratio: 7:1 vs. 8:1 vs. 9:1 — three different figures, two of them the
school's own.**
- **7:1** — 2025–26 School Profile ("Fast Facts")
- **8:1** — davidsonday.org Upper School page
- **9:1** — PrivateSchoolReview (third party, school-reported via verified update)
The 7:1 and 8:1 conflict is **between two of the school's own live sources**. 7:1 is likely
whole-school and 8:1 Upper-School-specific, but neither page says so. Flagged, not resolved.

**3. Total enrollment: 541 vs. 572.**
The 2025–26 School Profile states **541**. PrivateSchoolReview states **572**. The Profile is the
primary source and is dated; the aggregator figure may be from a different year. Use 541 and note
the conflict.

**4. AP course count: 21 (my derivation) vs. 23 (PrivateSchoolReview).**
The school **publishes no AP count at all**. My **21** counts distinct College Board AP courses in
the Profile's AP column, excluding the 4 marked "not offered in 25-26" and excluding Calculus III
(post-AP, not a College Board course). PrivateSchoolReview says **"23 AP/advanced courses"** but
lists only 7 by name, and its category is "AP/advanced," which is not the same set. These do not
reconcile. Both shown; **21 is the defensible figure** because it is derived from the school's own
current course table.

**5. Global Studies language requirement: 4 years vs. Level IV / 3 levels.**
- **School Profile:** Global Studies students must complete "the same world language in **all four
  years** of upper school."
- **Global Studies Diploma page:** "Completion of **Level IV or 3 levels** of the same language in
  Upper School."
These are **materially different requirements** — four years of seat time vs. reaching Level IV
(achievable in three years by a student who placed in above Level I). Both are current
davidsonday.org sources. Flagged; the Diploma page is more specific and more likely operative,
but neither is silently chosen.

**6. Blended Top-25 ranking corrections vs. the in-repo dossier.**
- **U.S. Air Force Academy:** the prior dossier cited **#5 LAC** in one place and omitted it from
  the Top-25 table. Verified 2026: **#5 LAC** (per the Air Force Academy Association's own

  blended Top-25**, and the prior table was wrong to omit it. Note one secondary search summary
  reported AFA at #7; the USAFA-sourced figure of **#5** is used and the #7 sighting is flagged
  here as unreliable.
- **Vassar College:** one secondary source reported **#12**; the more detailed 2026 breakdown

- **Smith College (#13 LAC), Notre Dame (#20 Nat.), Emory (#24 Nat.):** all three appeared in the

  than a padded 25. `TO VERIFY`.

**7. AFAR participation: "hundreds" vs. "more than 150" vs. "more than 200."**
Three legitimate sources give three figures over three different windows (goafar.org "hundreds"
since 2007; Charlotte Magazine "more than 150" to Cahal Pech; MDPI *Heritage* "more than 200"
