- The Academic Profile is dated **2025–26** and its test-score and AP blocks describe the
  **Class of 2025** (a graduated class). The most recent class with published outcomes is the

### Mandatory-exam policy

Verbatim from the Academic Profile, "ADVANCED PLACEMENT POLICIES":

> "All students enrolled in AP courses are required to take the corresponding AP exam."

Paired enforcement mechanism — also verbatim:

> "Students scoring below 70 in an AP course will not receive additional GPA weight. Prior to
> the AP exam, they will also be required to take a teacher designed exam that will be averaged
> in as part of the second semester grade."

AP admission is gated, not open: "Students are admitted into AP courses based on grades in
prerequisite courses, teacher recommendations, PSAT/SAT/ACT scores, demonstrated motivation and
a capacity to learn independently."

### Lever 2 — The school's leverage

**The School Profile as an instrument.**
The 2025–26 Academic Profile is an unusually *disclosive* document, and that is itself the
leverage. It voluntarily hands admissions readers:
- a **full per-subject AP score distribution** (31 subjects, every score band) — most schools
  publish only headline percentages;
- a **quintile GPA table with mean SAT and mean ACT per fifth**, which lets a reader place any
  applicant in the class distribution *despite* the no-rank policy;
- explicit **AP-load norms** ("two for sophomores, three for juniors, four for seniors"), which
  define what "most rigorous" means at this school so a reader cannot penalize a student for
  taking a normal load;
- the **weighting formula** (.5 Advanced / 1.0 AP) and the note that end-of-course grades on the
  transcript are **unweighted** — so readers can recompute.

This is a school choosing to be legible rather than opaque. It converts a no-rank policy from a
liability into context.

## Card — Verdict & Visit Checklist

### Why it holds up — evidence-backed verdict points

1. **The transcript is engineered to be readable, and that is rare.** Providence Day does not rank
   students, but it publishes a **quintile table with mean weighted GPA, mean SAT, and mean ACT for
   each fifth** (4.467/1445/31 down to 2.930/1053/19), the **exact weighting formula** (.5 Advanced,
   1.0 AP, unweighted grades shown on the transcript), and **explicit AP-load norms** (2 sophomore /
   3 junior / 4 senior). An admissions reader can place any applicant in the class without a rank.
   Most no-rank schools publish far less.

2. **AP results are strong *and* fully disclosed at the subject level.** 1,213 exams in May 2025
   with **94% scoring 3+ and 77% scoring 4–5**, and the school prints the **complete per-subject
   score distribution for all 31 subjects** — including the weak spots (CS Principles: 41 threes,
   16 twos, 2 ones out of 110). Combined with a **mandatory-exam policy** ("All students enrolled in
   AP courses are required to take the corresponding AP exam"), the 94% is earned on a
   non-self-selected denominator, not a filtered one.

3. **The counseling office is deep, veteran, and genuinely high-touch.** Five credentialed
   counselors for **730 Upper School students / 178 seniors** (~36 seniors per counselor), each
   handling one fifth of every class, led by a director with **18+ years in the seat**, with
   associate directors who came from inside university admissions (UC Berkeley). Add **~150 reps
   hosted annually** and **50+ campuses visited by counselors** — and the office meets reps even
   when no students sign up, which is a relationship-maintenance choice.

4. **Outcomes are real at both ends of the class.** All **8 of 8 Ivies** appear on the 2023–2026
   acceptance list and **all eight are bolded** as multi-enrollee, alongside Stanford, MIT, Caltech,
   Duke, and Chicago. Simultaneously, accessible in-state options (App State, ECU, UNC–Charlotte,
   Western Carolina, High Point, **Central Piedmont Community College**) are *also* bolded. The
   ~380-institution list matches the school's actual quintile spread rather than flattering only
   the top fifth.

5. **Named-scholarship pipelines are numbered, not anecdotal.** The Class of 2025 produced
   Providence Day's **18th Morehead-Cain Scholar** — an explicitly-counted, decades-deep pipeline —
   plus a QuestBridge Scholar at Stanford, 2 full-tuition ROTC recipients, and a West Point
   appointment. The Class of 2026 followed with **$21M+ in merit offers to 82% of seniors**, 9
   National Merit Semifinalists (**all 9 advancing to Finalist**), and 18 Commended.

6. **Institutional integrity is stated in writing.** The profile commits to **NACAC's Guide to
   Ethical Practice**, and states the school **"reports all student behavioral probations,
   suspensions and expulsions through time of graduation"** to any college that asks — backed by a
   student-run Honor Council. Schools that self-report discipline are schools whose recommendations
   carry weight, and that credibility accrues to every applicant.

7. **Every graduate leaves with an experiential credential.** A **required 30-hour Senior Project
   internship** for all seniors, plus two elective signature diplomas — the **Global Studies
   Diploma** ("first of its kind in the country," 344 graduates since 2007, 23 in the Class of 2026)
   and the new-in-2026 **Diploma in Entrepreneurial Studies** under IDEAS@PD.

### Ask on the tour — questions targeting what the school does NOT publish

1. **"Your profile shows the fifth quintile averaging a 1053 SAT and a 2.930 weighted GPA. Where
   did those ~35 students actually enroll last year?"** — The school publishes the quintile *inputs*
   but never the *outcomes by quintile*. The ~380-name list is a four-year aggregate that cannot be
   sliced. This is the single biggest gap in their disclosure, and the answer tells you whether the
   bottom 40% is genuinely served or just listed.

2. **"Is every application read before submission, and do you have a stated ED/EA strategy?"** —
   Providence Day publishes essay workshops, a Senior Application Bootcamp, and fall individual
   meetings, but **never states** whether counselors formally review applications pre-submission or
   how they advise on binding early rounds. For a five-counselor office with ~36 seniors each, ask
   what the actual review commitment is.

3. **"What is your position on test-optional, and what share of last year's class actually
   submitted scores?"** — 143 SAT and 159 ACT testers out of 176 graduates suggests near-universal
   testing, but the school publishes **no test-optional policy and no submission rate**. Ask whether
   they counsel withholding, and at what score threshold.

4. **"The published counselor count says 'five' in one place and 'four' in another, and Darren
   Morgan isn't on the profile's contact block. Who exactly is on the team this year, and what is
   the current senior caseload?"** — The school's own two pages disagree, and the printed profile
   lists only four of the five names. Caseload is the number that matters.

5. **"How are testing accommodations documented for College Board and ACT, and how many Upper
   School students currently have them?"** — Student Services is described consultatively
   ("resource consultant," "self-advocates"), with **no published LD program structure, no
   accommodations counts, and no speech services**. If learning support matters, this is
   underspecified. Also confirm whether occupational therapy is billed directly to families.

6. **"The Entrepreneurial Studies Diploma is new for 2026 — what exactly does it require, and how
   many students have completed it?"** — The IDEAS page announces the diploma but publishes **no
   course requirements, capstone, or internship structure**, and directs inquiries to an email
   address. Compare against Global Studies, which has 344 graduates and published requirements.

7. **"Your 2025–26 profile still reports Class of 2024 National Merit numbers and a 2022–2025
   acceptance list, while the website shows 2023–2026. When is the profile refreshed, and what are
   the current Class of 2026 AP and testing numbers?"** — The flagship document colleges receive is
   a cycle behind the website on at least three metrics. Ask which version reps are getting.

8. **"Your admissions page says $23 million in merit scholarships for the Class of 2025; the
   official profile says over $20 million. Which is it, and what's counted in each?"** — A direct
   test of data discipline, and worth asking plainly.
