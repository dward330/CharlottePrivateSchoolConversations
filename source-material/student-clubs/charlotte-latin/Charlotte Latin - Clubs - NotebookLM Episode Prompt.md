## "Inside Charlotte Latin Clubs & Activities"

### PROMPT TO PASTE INTO NOTEBOOKLM

You are producing a single podcast episode titled **"Inside Charlotte Latin Clubs & Activities."** This episode covers student clubs and activities at Charlotte Latin School **outside of athletics and the arts**, across the Lower, Middle, and Upper Schools (transitional kindergarten through twelfth grade).

**Open the episode** with the host stating the full agenda in plain language before any discussion begins. Say something like: "Today we're walking through Charlotte Latin's clubs and activities in six parts: first, the full catalog of clubs and what they are; second, how many students take part and which clubs are most popular; third, the bigger themes these clubs build toward; fourth, the clubs that actually compete against other schools; fifth, service, leadership, and civic life; and sixth, how clubs are run, started, funded, and found."

Then work through the **six segments in this exact order:**

1. **The Club Catalog — What's on Offer.** Walk through the landscape: 25 in-scope Upper School clubs, a Middle School model where students rotate through 25-plus offerings every 8-to-10 weeks, and a Lower School where club life runs mostly through after-school enrichment rather than named clubs. Read off a representative spread of the actual Upper School clubs by name — for example Chess Club, Model U N, Math Club, the National Junior Classical League, Mosaic, World Quest, the Outdoor Club, and the service clubs — so listeners hear the real breadth, not just a couple of examples. Note clearly that performing- and literary-arts clubs (a cappella, improv, the thespian society, the literary journal, and creative writing) are set aside because the series covers non-arts activities. [source: Club Catalog & Descriptions]

2. **Participation & Popularity.** Be honest and specific here: the School does not publish a hard participation rate for non-sports clubs, so don't invent one. Explain the strongest real signal — that Middle School participation is near-universal *by design* because club time is built into the schedule, and Upper School clubs meet during the school day. Mention the School's named most-popular Middle School clubs (Cooking, Just Dance, Robotics, Student Council, Sports Club). When you mention the outside survey showing 94 percent positive sentiment, immediately add that it came from only 17 responses, so it's a mood reading, not a real participation number. [source: Participation & Popularity]

3. **Thematic Pathways — What Clubs Build Toward.** Explain how clubs cluster into pathways: a civic and public-voice pathway (debate, Model U N, World Quest, student government), a global and cultural pathway (language clubs, the classics league, and the sister-school travel program), a S-T-E-M and innovation pathway anchored by the M-I-T-affiliated Fab Lab, and a narrower entrepreneurship thread. Frame these as *exposure* — joining Model U N exposes a student to diplomacy and research; it is not a guarantee of any outcome. [source: Thematic Pathways]

4. **The Clubs That Compete.** This is where Charlotte Latin shines. Lead with Speech and Debate as the flagship — a top-twenty program nationally per the N-S-D-A, with charter status and 200 Club recognition, and recent top finishes at the national tournament. Then cover Model U N, Science Olympiad (Division B in Middle School and Division C in Upper School, reaching the state tournament in 2026), World Quest, and the Math Team. Be careful: say the Science Olympiad team *reached* the state tournament, but do not claim a specific placement, because the public results don't confirm one. Also note plainly what's *not* there — no mock trial, academic decathlon, or business-competition chapter shows up in the School's materials. [source: Academic & Competitive Programs]

5. **Service, Leadership & Civic Life.** Describe the largest cluster: a Service Council that organizes weekly service, a slate of service clubs (Club Sandwich, Blessings in a Backpack, Paws for the Cause), and a recognition pathway — the Service Society for students who top 150 service hours. Cover the governance bodies: the elected Student Council, the Honor Council and its Advisory Board, and the Hawks Lead leadership program. Then the affinity and advocacy clubs: Mosaic (which houses Q-and-A and the Black Student Association), SISTERS, Girl Up, and S-A-D-D. [source: Service, Leadership & Civic Engagement]

6. **How Clubs Are Run, Started, Funded & Found — and Closing Takeaways.** Explain that clubs are advisor-anchored, meet during the school day, and that students are encouraged to start their own with a faculty advisor. Be candid about the gaps: the School doesn't publish a detailed new-club approval process, doesn't publish club budgets, and there's no confirmed all-School club fair — though the Middle School rotation effectively *is* a discovery engine. Close with honest takeaways: Charlotte Latin's clubs are strongest in the civic/public-voice and S-T-E-M arenas, the service culture is deep and well-documented, and a few operational details simply aren't public. [source: Governance, Formation, Funding & Discovery]

**Tone:** Conversational and balanced, grounded strictly in the uploaded sources. Honor every caveat the dossiers flag — no outcome promises ("looks great for college" becomes "exposure and demonstrated interest"), the small-sample caveat on the 94%/82% survey numbers, the "reached state but no confirmed placement" note for Science Olympiad, and the funding/club-fair gaps. Do not invent club names, membership numbers, or results. Do not reference any other school.
