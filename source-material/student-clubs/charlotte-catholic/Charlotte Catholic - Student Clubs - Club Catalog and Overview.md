**Provenance**

- **Compiled by:** Claude Code deep research pass (`/implement add-charlotte-catholic`).
- **Date compiled:** 2026-08-18
- **Method:** The clubs directory was downloaded as **raw HTML** and parsed by matching the
  page's Finalsite `fsElementTitle` blocks, so every club, its staff advisor, its room and
  its description come from the markup itself rather than from a summarizer's count. **A
  page-summarizer reading of the same page returned 106 clubs — an overcount** produced by

  the true total of 76.** Honor-society criteria and the service requirement were
  transcribed from the **Student Handbook 2025-2026** via `pdftotext -layout`.

## Community service — a graduation requirement

From the Student Handbook (S2), verbatim in substance:

- **Annual requirement:** each year, students in grades 9–12 complete and submit service
  hours, **with at least 5 hours designated as Parish Service**. **Freshmen submit 10
  hours; sophomores, juniors and seniors submit 20 hours each year.**
- **Graduation requirement:** **70 approved service hours over four years.** (The total is
  adjusted if a student is enrolled fewer than four years.)
- **Submission deadline:** hours must be submitted by **March 1** each year to be logged
  and approved.
- **Documentation:** students document the activity on Canvas; pictures may be included.
- **Campus Ministry assists** students in finding service opportunities.

Because service is a condition of graduation, the school profile's claim of **100%
community service participation** is structurally true rather than a survey result.

## ⚠️ Publications and media — CCHS DOES have student publications

An earlier reconnaissance pass concluded that CCHS has no student media, because the
student handbook contains no publications section and the site has no publications page.
**The curriculum guide refutes that.** The **Journalism (234)** course description states
that students "are responsible for producing and publishing **the school newspaper** in
digital format on the school's website," that they "will also develop and publish **the
school's literary magazine**," and that they have the opportunity to **produce a podcast**.
The **Yearbook (235)** course description covers producing **the school yearbook**, with
selected students serving as photographers.

Both are year-long electives for grades 10–12 requiring a B average in English and a
teacher recommendation (Yearbook additionally requires an application and an interview).
**So the school has a newspaper, a literary magazine, a yearbook and a podcast** — all
produced through the English department rather than as standalone clubs, which is why they
are absent from the clubs directory. The app records them under Publications & Media.

## Flagship competitive result — the honest position

The strongest documented competitive result is **Speech & Debate: North Carolina state
runner-up, with Darlene Singui-Tanyi the Program Oral Interpretation State Champion** —
but that coverage is from **2017** (S6), nine years old and older than anything shipped for
any other school on this roster. **It is not used as the flagship result.** The app instead
uses the **2025 Blumey Awards finalists** (five finalists, one in each of five categories),
which is recent, dated and independently verifiable.
