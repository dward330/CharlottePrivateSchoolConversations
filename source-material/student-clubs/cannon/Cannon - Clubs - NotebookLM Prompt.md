You are creating a podcast episode titled "Inside Cannon School: Clubs & Activities." Two friendly, well-prepared co-hosts talk through what Cannon School in Concord, North Carolina gives students beyond the classroom and the playing field: its clubs, student leadership, signature competitive teams, honor societies, and service. Keep the tone conversational and balanced, and ground every claim in the source dossiers. Where the research flags a gap, say so plainly instead of guessing or filling space.

Open by having one host lay out the full agenda in plain language: how clubs work across all three divisions; the Upper School club lineup; Middle School clubs and academic teams; Lower School activities; student governance; the signature competitive programs; honor societies; service and community engagement; and affinity and identity groups. Right up front, explain that sports and the arts are not covered in this episode because each of those has its own dedicated episode in this series.

Segment 1, how club life works: Cannon's clubs are student-led and faculty-supported, and students can start a new club whenever an interest isn't already represented, so the lineup shifts year to year. [overview]

Segment 2, Upper School clubs: walk through the organizations Cannon names directly, including affinity groups, the Habitat service club, the Star Wars Club, and the student yearbook, The Flashback. Be clear this is a representative snapshot, not a complete chartered list, because the school doesn't publish one. [US catalog]

Segment 3, Middle School: read the academic competition teams in full, which are Battle of the Books, Elementary Battle of the Books, Science Olympiad, Model UN, and Envirothon. Mention the Middle School Student Council and advisory traditions like Pumpkin Chunkin and the Big Wheel Derby. [MS]

Segment 4, Lower School: cover the Lower School Student Council, the Junior Kindergarten to Senior Buddy program, Adaptive Expertise Days with student-chosen passion classes such as astronomy and woodworking, and signature events like Olympic Day and the fourth-grade Living History Museum. [LS]

Segment 5, governance: three elected student councils across the divisions, a student-elected Honor Council tied to the yearly Honor Code and Unity Pledge, and the four-year, advisory-based House System. [governance]

Segment 6, signature competitive programs: lead with the flagship, the Brainy Yaks, a Middle School First LEGO League robotics team of sixth through tenth graders that won the state Champions Award and finished in the top 100 out of more than 32,000 teams at the First LEGO League World Championship in Houston in back-to-back years, with a recent project on coral-reef restoration backed by three campus makerspaces. Then esports, Cannon School Gaming, which grew out of student LAN parties and now runs from its own dedicated lab with titles like Rocket League and League of Legends, supported by a Business of Esports course. After that, the DECA business team, which reached finalist standing at its 2026 conference, and the Model UN team, which competed at the Duke conference. Report the robotics result plainly and don't inflate it into "elite" or "powerhouse" language. [competitive]

Segment 7, honor societies: the Cum Laude Society chapter dating to 2005, the new student-founded Science National Honor Society inducted in 2025, and discipline societies in math, social studies, English, journalism, and the world languages. [honor societies]

Segment 8, service: students log roughly 15,000 service hours a year even though service is not a graduation requirement, through senior Capstone projects that students present to the school, the Upper School House Day of Service, and Middle School service-learning partnerships with groups like the Special Olympics of Cabarrus County and a local gleaning network. [service]

Segment 9, affinity and identity groups: be candid here. Sources confirm affinity groups are active and specifically name Black affinity groups, tied to the school's Diversity, Belongingness, and Engagement commitment, but Cannon doesn't publish a full named list, so don't invent one. [affinity]

Closing: pull together the throughline, a student-driven culture where kids build the programs they want. Then close sincerely by thanking listeners and acknowledging that if any club or activity was left out, it's only because it didn't surface in our research, and no slight is intended.
