## A grade-by-grade service ladder with 12 named partners

This is a **curricular** programme, not a volunteer club — every grade has a standing
partnership tied to its curriculum.

The school's framing, verbatim:
> "Regular and meaningful service activity in our local and global community is intended
> to open children's eyes to the broader world, develop service as a habit of the heart
> and intentionally challenge students to step out of their comfort zones."

> "Each grade level partners with a community organization whose mission and services are
> related to curricular goals… **Year-long partnerships create the possibility of
> relationship formation that transforms charity into mutual service among neighbors.**"

| Grade | Partner(s) | Theme / detail |
|---|---|---|
| **K** | Nourish Up | Hunger and social isolation; students deliver **Meals on Wheels** weekly |
| **1** | Nourish Up | Visit the **Nourish Up Food Pantry at First Presbyterian Church**; lead a school **Food Drive in November**; deliver groceries with parents |
| **2** | InReach | Adults with intellectual/developmental disabilities; a self-advocacy class at the **Baxter St. community center** and **Covenant Presbyterian Church**; InReach customers visit Trinity **monthly** for gardening, art, music and self-advocacy |
| **3** | **Roof Above** / Urban Ministry Center | Homelessness; students **walk a mile to the Urban Ministry Center** monthly for art, soccer, **singing with the Urban Ministry Choir**, and clothing drives |
| **4** | The Epiphany School of Charlotte | Children with physical, cognitive and social differences; reciprocal relationships |
| **5** | Galilee Ministries of East Charlotte | "Housed in the former St. Andrew's Episcopal Church on Central Avenue… a special mission of the Episcopal Diocese of North Carolina… services to refugees, immigrants, migrants, and neighbors in East Charlotte." |
| **6** | 8th Street Friendship Garden · Charlotte Community ToolBank · NourishUp | **"Feeding Our Neighbors"** — crop rotations, growth cycles, food scarcity |
| **7** | Crisis Assistance Ministry · Hope Haven · First Ward Child Development Center | **"Understanding Our Neighbors"** — houselessness, addiction, poverty, human trafficking |
| **8** | *(no named partner)* | **"Advocacy and Social Action"** — community mapping, a Social Issues Seminar research project, and a **capstone advocacy trip to Washington, DC** |

**12 named partner organizations** across grades K–7.

## The uptown location is integral to the programme

From the Head of School's welcome page, verbatim:

> "We like to say that **Trinity has the longest hallways in Charlotte** as our campus
> community extends into the heart of Uptown Charlotte. Each day you will see our students
> and teachers walking to and engaging with community organizations as part of our
> academic program and service-learning partnerships."

> ⚠️ This quote is on **`/about/`**, NOT the service-learning page. Cite it correctly.

This is the mechanism behind the grade-3 partnership — students **walk** to the Urban
Ministry Center. A 4.5-acre uptown campus is what makes a walking service programme
possible, and the school says so explicitly.

## The 8th-grade capstone — an advocacy trip to Washington, DC

From the May 2024 account (threadid=15): **46 eighth-graders** travelled to Washington.

Assistant Head of School **Stephanie Griffin**, verbatim:
> "This is a trip, at its heart and soul, that's all about advocacy."

Topics students advocated on: **gun-violence victim resources, economic mobility, minimum
wage, affordable housing, and healthcare cost transparency**.

They met **Rep. Jeff Jackson** at the U.S. Capitol, plus staff for Reps. Alma Adams, Dan
Bishop, Deborah Ross and Bobby Scott, and Sens. Budd, Tillis, Capito, Hickenlooper, Brown
and Murphy. They also met **Moms Demand Action**, the **U.S. Department of Labor**, and
the **National Low Income Housing Coalition**.

The trip is tied to the **Social Issues Seminar (8)**, a weekly enrichment — so the
capstone is curricular, not extracurricular.

Related civic programming in the blog: an 8th-grade advocacy panel, a transit-vote
exercise giving 8th grade "a glimpse at the election process", and students witnessing a
**Walk for Peace** during a Charlotte visit.
