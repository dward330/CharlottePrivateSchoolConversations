  1. 1
    Visit — an open house or information sessionFall 2026

    The Montessori Open House is Wednesday, October 21 at 9:30 a.m., and a Kindergarten Virtual Information Session runs Thursday, November 12 at 12:00 p.m. Ages are set by August 1: 2 for Pre-Primary, 3 for Primary, 4 for TK, and 5 for Montessori K or Traditional K.

  2. 2
    Apply through FACTS and pay the $100 feeby Jan 2

    Start the application in the FACTS admissions portal; the $100 application fee is non-refundable. January 2 is the priority deadline — two weeks ahead of Grades 1–8.

  3. 3
    Teacher recommendationsby Feb 1

    Enter your child’s current teacher’s email in the portal and the recommendation form goes to them online. The school does not state how many recommendations it needs.

  4. 4
    Kindergarten testing — the WPPSI-IV through CAISby Feb 1

    Montessori K and Kindergarten applicants take the WPPSI-IV through the CAIS consortium, booked with one psychologist only. "External testing is not required before Montessori Kindergarten," so Pre-Primary, Primary and TK applicants sit no test. CAIS also requires an interview with each child, typically November–February.

  5. 5
    Meet and Greet, or Kindergarten Play DayWinter

    Montessori applicants come for a Meet and Greet of "about 20-30 minutes". Kindergarten applicants who apply by the Round 1 deadline are invited to a Kindergarten Play Day.

  6. 6
    Decision → contractFeb 26, 2027

    Decisions are emailed on February 26, 2027 at 4:00 p.m., and the enrollment contract is due March 5 by noon, with a $2,000 down payment credited to tuition.

Running in parallel: the financial-aid clock

Tuition assistance is offered for Kindergarten through 8th grade, through Clarity, on "financial need, academic merit, and the student’s fit". The school’s aid page gives two deadlines — January 2 in its body and January 15 in its FAQ — so confirm the date with the Director of Enrollment before relying on either. Pre-Primary, Primary and TK are not eligible for aid.

Student Clubs

Service & Civic EngagementService is built into the leadership program: two annual service days, a cross-age buddy system that pairs every Lower Schooler with an older student, and student-led drives.

Service is built into the leadership program: two annual service days, a cross-age buddy system that pairs every Lower Schooler with an older student, and student-led drives. The school states five goals for it, from "nurture compassion and empathy for others" to "develop a life-long commitment to service and our society". "The leadership curriculum expounds on the idea of servant leadership and the importance of civic engagement."

2
annual service days
The Family Day of Service and the Student Morning of Service

On the Family Day of Service, "Prep families do service projects for local organizations" on and off campus. On the Student Morning of Service, "students engage in service work that addresses childhood literacy, child homelessness, animal welfare, homelessness, or economic inequality." Alongside both, the Parents Association’s holiday drive, Preppy’s Pantry, "collects coats and non-perishable food items", and Lower School students "routinely visit a local nursing home to read and play games with residents."

SOURCE charlotteprep.org — Service Learning ↗
BUDEE
"Be Understanding, Dedicated, Empathetic, and Engaged"
A buddy for every Lower Schooler

BUDEE "matches TK students from the Early School with 3rd Graders and each of the other Lower School students with a Middle School buddy", and the pairs "meet for community service, character education, and teamwork exercises." In 2023–24 the BUDEE groups ran programs for the International Day of Peace, Veterans Day, Hispanic Heritage Month, a Day of Service, Earth Day and more. Eighth graders also tutor their 4th-grade BUDEEs.

Student-led relief, and a 2nd-grade tradition

In October 2024 the student council ran a carpool-line supply drive for Hurricane Helene relief, every class made cards of hope, and the school co-hosted a benefit concert with 7th Street Concerts and the Sphinx Virtuosi; students "filled a moving truck with essential goods" and delivered it to the YMCA. Each February, second graders host a Black History Lunch & Learn for Charlotte community leaders — the seventh annual one was February 12, 2026.

The process, in sequence
Spring of 7th grade — the Parent Coffee
Orientation
"Each spring, rising 8th-grade parents attend a Parent Coffee to learn about the admissions process, events, deadlines, and other tips and information to prepare for the year ahead."
Grade 7 — a practice ISEE
Testing
Students sit the Independent School Entrance Exam for practice in 7th grade, then for high school placement in 8th, and the school provides ISEE test preparation in between: "Students learn test-taking strategies and prepare for admissions testing to gain comfort and fine tune skills."
September of 8th grade — the High School Fair
Exposure
"In September, administrators from over 40 schools are invited to Charlotte Prep's High School Fair and Boarding School Fair to recruit our students." The boarding fair includes a panel discussion — one 2026 family credits it with their decision to pursue boarding school.
Fall of 8th grade — meetings and school visits
Fit
The team meets individually with each 8th grader and separately with each student's parents "to discuss their plans, their objectives for their child, and appropriate placement opportunities." Families are encouraged to visit open houses and tours, and students make day visits to the schools on their list.
Grade 8 — interviews
Preparation
"In Leadership class, students learn interview etiquette and conduct. They learn about proper dress, eye contact, handshake, and what to look for as they seek the school that fits them best." Teachers and administrators run mock interviews, and the brochure adds a practice interview with the head of school.
Winter of 8th grade — applications and advocacy
Advocacy
"Charlotte Prep electronically delivers our students' complete transcripts and teacher recommendation packets to the schools prior to deadlines." The team then meets with each high school "for in-depth conversations about our student applicants and to provide further insight to ensure each student is well known."
