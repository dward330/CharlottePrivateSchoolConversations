## The math track — a real published sequence with five 8th-grade endpoints

Verbatim:
> "Students are placed in grade-level and accelerated courses based on **specific
> performance criteria**. Instruction is grounded in a problem-based core curriculum
> rooted in content and practice standards to foster learning and achievement for all."

> "The following courses are offered:
> **6th Grade:** Math 6
> **7th Grade:** Pre-Algebra and Advanced Pre-Algebra
> **8th Grade:** Foundations in Algebra, Math 1, Algebra 1, Math 2, Geometry"

| Grade | Courses |
|---|---|
| 6 | Math 6 |
| 7 | Pre-Algebra · Advanced Pre-Algebra |
| 8 | Foundations in Algebra · Math 1 · **Algebra 1** · Math 2 · **Geometry** |

**Five distinct 8th-grade math courses** is genuinely differentiated for a middle school,
and it is the clearest "advanced work" signal this school publishes.

> ⚠️ **The placement criteria themselves are NOT published.** The page says placement is
> on "specific performance criteria" and never states what they are. That full sentence is
> the entirety of what exists. **Do not imply the criteria are available.**

## World language — the one genuine high-school-credit claim

Two separate verbatim statements:

> "Middle School students choose between **Latin and Spanish**, completing **Level 1, a
> high school course**, by the end of their 8th Grade year."

> "Students will graduate from Trinity having completed **Spanish I, a high school level
> course**, and will be ready for **Spanish II** should they choose to continue studying
> the language."

Note the two phrasings differ slightly — the World Language header says "a high school
course", the Spanish paragraph says "a high school **level** course". Quote whichever fits
the context, exactly as published.

**This is the K–8 analogue of "advanced courses"**: a student leaves 8th grade having
completed a high-school Level 1 language.
