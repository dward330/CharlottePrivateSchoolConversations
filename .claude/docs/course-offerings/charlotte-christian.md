## Upper School (Grades 9–12) — source S1

### Biblical Studies (4 units required — one every year)

- **Old Testament Survey** — *Gr 9 · Required* — Covers the historical flow of the Old Testament, acquainting students with the background, content, and message of each book and the unity of all books in the progress of revelation and redemption.
- **New Testament Survey** — *Gr 10 · Required* — An overview of the New Testament books covering the validity of Scripture, geography of Bible lands, the life of Messiah Jesus, the actions of Jesus' first disciples, and lessons in the letters.
- **Christian Theology** — *Gr 11 · Semester* — Surveys the essential doctrines of the Christian faith using both systematic and historical theology to answer the question "What is a Christian?"
- **World Religions** — *Gr 11 · Semester* — Trains students to analyze and evaluate the truth claims of other faith systems using Scripture and reason, examining ancient, Eastern, Judaic, and Islamic religions plus cults.
- **Apologetics and Cultural Engagement** — *Gr 12 · Required* — Equips students to articulate and live out their Christian faith, with first semester on apologetics and second on biblical foundations for cultural engagement.

### English (4 units required)

- **English 9** — *Gr 9* — Introduces high-school-level critical thinking, reading comprehension, research, and writing through a Survey of Genres spanning mythology, Gothic, sci-fi/fantasy, short story, nonfiction, satire, poetry, and drama.
- **English 9 Honors** — *Honors* — Differs from English 9 in intensity, pace, and depth of study, with more reading, independent work, and complex writing assignments.
- **English 10** — *Gr 10* — A Survey of American Literature built on the essential questions "what does it mean to be an American" and "what does 'freedom' mean," studied from a biblical perspective.
- **English 10 Honors** — *Honors* — Differs from English 10 in intensity, pace, and depth, demanding mastery of abstract concepts.
- **English 11** — *Gr 11* — A Survey of World Literature asking "What does it mean to be an engaged global citizen?" through classic and modern literature of Latin America, Africa, Asia, and beyond.
- **English 11 Honors** — *Honors* — Differs from English 11 in pace and depth with focused intensity on fine-tuning already strong writing skills.
- **English 12** — *Gr 12* — A Survey of European Literature and Legacy examining adulthood, choices, and consequences from Greek and Roman classics through modern Europe and colonial legacy.
- **English 12 Honors** — *Honors* — Differs from English 12 in pace and depth with focused intensity on refining strong writing skills.
- **Advanced Placement English Language and Composition** — *AP* — An intensive "how to" course teaching students to read purposefully for complexity and nuance and write persuasively for varied audiences and contexts.
- **Advanced Placement English Literature and Composition** — *AP* — Focuses on reading, analyzing, and writing about imaginative literature — fiction, poetry, and drama — from various periods.
- **Introduction to Public Speaking** — *Semester · Gr 9-12* — Equips students with practical techniques to control the fear of public speaking through body language and voice, fulfilling the speech graduation requirement.
- **Children's Literature and Film Study** — *Semester · Gr 9-12* — A general survey of children's literature from early childhood books to young adult novels, pairing original texts with their film versions.
- **Creative Writing** — *Semester · Gr 9-12* — A workshop course spanning poetry, short story, humor and satire, personal essay, memoir, and creative nonfiction, from origination of ideas to publication.
- **Sci-Fi and Fantasy: Literature and Film through a Biblical Lens** — *Semester · Gr 10-12* — Explores classic science fiction and fantasy — Lewis, Tolkien, Rowling — examining themes, symbolism, and allegory from a Christian worldview.
- **Dystopian Literature and Film through a Biblical Lens** — *Semester · Gr 10-12* — Examines Orwell, Huxley, and Collins through a Christian lens to analyze what happens when societies reject God to build a "perfect" world.
- **Poetry Workshop, Honors** — *Honors · Semester · Gr 10-12* — A rigorous course in both the creation and critical analysis of poetry, covering rhythm, imagery, metaphor, and structure in a workshop setting.

### Mathematics (4 units required)

- **Algebra I** — *Year* — Reviews pre-algebra skills and introduces integers, functions and graphs, equations and inequalities, linear and quadratic functions, exponents, radicals, and polynomials.
- **Geometry** — *Year* — A thorough introduction to classical Euclidean geometry emphasizing deductive reasoning via lines, angles, triangles, circles, polygons, and solid figures.
- **Geometry Honors** — *Honors* — Covers Euclidean geometry in a more formal, proof-centered approach at a more rigorous and faster pace.
- **Algebra II** — *Year* — Study of the real number system emphasizing functions and their properties, including matrices, polynomial, exponential, logarithmic, and rational functions.
- **Algebra II & Trigonometry** — *Year* — Covers the Algebra II function content plus probability and a deeper understanding of trigonometric concepts.
- **Algebra II & Trigonometry Honors** — *Honors* — A fast-paced course taking a deep look at the real number system, functions, probability, and trigonometry in detail.
- **Pre-Calculus** — *Year* — Further development of modeling and applying linear, polynomial, exponential, trigonometric, power, and logarithmic functions plus sequences and series.
- **Pre-Calculus Honors** — *Honors* — For students who enjoy mathematics without the depth or stress of an AP course, adding analytic trigonometry, vectors, polar equations, and an introduction to limits.
- **Advanced Placement Pre-Calculus** — *AP* — Covers the topics of a freshman college algebra course with in-depth study of functions, analytic trigonometry, vectors, and discrete mathematics.
- **Advanced Placement Calculus AB** — *AP* — A college-level course following a first-semester college calculus curriculum covering limits, derivatives, and integrals; single-blocked.
- **Advanced Placement Calculus BC** — *AP* — Follows first- and second-semester college calculus, adding sequences, improper integrals, infinite series, and parametric, vector, and polar functions; double-blocked.
- **Probability, Statistics and Finite Mathematics** — *Year* — Develops statistics — collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data — plus an elementary introduction to probability, reinforced with real-life projects.
- **Probability, Statistics and Finite Mathematics Honors** — *Honors* — A fast-paced treatment of statistics and probability including distributions, statistical estimation and testing, confidence intervals, and linear regression.
- **Advanced Placement Statistics** — *AP* — Examines and simulates probability distributions to predict and estimate future events, analyzing relationships between variables via correlation and regression.
- **Calculus III** — *Post-AP* — Follows AP Calculus with multivariable functions, vector-valued functions, curvature, double and triple integrals, vector analysis, and differential equations; carries AP GPA weighting.

### Science (3 units required: Biology, Chemistry, Physics)

- **Biology** — *Year* — Integrates class work with laboratory experience covering molecular life, cell structure and function, metabolism, genetics, classification, and theories about the origin of life.
- **Biology Honors** — *Honors* — For students with exceptional math and science skills, covering Biology topics with increased breadth and depth.
- **Chemistry** — *Year* — Explores the composition, structure, properties, and transformation of matter through atomic theory, stoichiometry, reactions, bonding, kinetics, and equilibrium.
- **Chemistry Honors** — *Honors* — For students with exceptional aptitude in math and science, covering topics with increased breadth, depth, and lab time.
- **Physics** — *Year* — A general overview of Newtonian mechanics plus electricity, magnetism, light, and sound, with heavy emphasis on hands-on laboratory work.
- **Physics Honors** — *Honors* — Investigates matter from subatomic particles to galaxies, training students to analyze and solve problems scientifically through classical mechanics, electricity, and magnetism.
- **Advanced Placement Physics I** — *AP* — Covers mechanics and fluids using an algebra-based approach, equivalent to the first semester of introductory college physics for non-science majors.
- **Advanced Placement Biology** — *AP · Alternate years* — Focuses on college-level biological principles and methodology, investigating cell structure and function, the cell cycle, genetics, and ecology in depth.
- **Advanced Placement Chemistry** — *AP · Alternate years* — The equivalent of a general college chemistry course, investigating topics in depth and at a brisk pace.
- **Advanced Placement Environmental Science** — *AP* — An interdisciplinary college-level course on ecosystems, human population dynamics, resource use, pollution, eutrophication, ozone depletion, and climate change.
- **Environmental Science Honors** — *Honors · Alternate years* — A hands-on interdisciplinary elective on the interrelationships of the natural world, identifying and analyzing environmental problems and evaluating solutions.
- **Astronomy** — *Semester · Gr 11-12* — Explores the cosmos including constellations, the solar system, radiation and matter, black holes, galaxies, dark matter, and large-scale structure.
- **Anatomy/Physiology** — *Year* — A survey of the structure, function, and interactive dynamics of the human body, with case studies and required dissections.
- **Forensic Science I** — *Semester · Gr 11-12* — Applies basic biological, chemical, and physical science principles to crime scene investigation, from the crime scene to the courtroom.
- **Forensic Science II Honors** — *Honors · Semester · Gr 11-12* — Applies advanced science principles to crime scenes, focusing on laws and evaluation of evidence to build a credible case through extensive lab work.

### Social Studies (4 units required)

- **World History I: Ancient Civilizations** — *Gr 9* — Studies geography, world religions, and major developments from early civilizations through the mid 1300–1400s.
- **World History I Honors: Ancient Civilizations** — *Honors* — Differs in intensity and depth, with primary-source analysis, document-based questions, and more writing to prepare for AP World History.
- **World History II: The World and the West** — *Year* — Surveys c. 1200 onward, exploring the political, religious, social, and ideological forces that shaped the modern world through the twentieth century.
- **World History II Honors: The World and the West** — *Honors* — Adds select honors-level topics, depth, and writing skills preparing students for future AP social studies courses.
- **Advanced Placement World History: Modern** — *AP* — A college-level survey of major civilizations, events, religions, and ideologies from c. 1200 through the end of the twentieth century.
- **United States History** — *Year* — Surveys American history from Native American settlements to the end of the twentieth century against the backdrop of world and current events.
- **United States History Honors** — *Honors* — Differs in intensity and depth, emphasizing examination of primary sources and writing assignments.
- **Advanced Placement United States History** — *AP* — Studies cultural, economic, political, and social developments shaping the U.S. from c. 1491 to the present through analysis of texts and historical evidence.
- **American Government** — *Semester* — Gives a broad-based understanding of the institutions and purposes of American government, foundational principles, the political process, separation of powers, and civil liberties.
- **American Government Honors** — *Honors · Semester* — Covers American government in greater depth and requires a semester research paper on the inner workings of government.
- **Economics** — *Semester* — Gives a broad-based understanding of the free-market economic system including macroeconomic and microeconomic elements and economic policy decisions.
- **Economics Honors** — *Honors · Semester* — Covers economics in greater depth and requires a semester research paper demonstrating deeper analysis of economic theory.
- **Advanced Placement United States Government & Politics** — *AP* — Gives a critical perspective on economics, politics, and government through general concepts and specific case studies of American political reality.
- **Psychology** — *Semester · Gr 9-12* — An appreciation of the science of psychology across self, body, mind, environment, mental health, and life, integrating a Christian perspective throughout.
- **Advanced Placement Psychology** — *AP · Gr 12* — Studies the historical development and contemporary discipline of psychology across biology, cognition, psychological functioning, and social functioning, analyzed from a biblical worldview.
- **Sociology** — *Semester · Gr 9-12* — Familiarizes students with various cultures and the problems resulting from people living in groups, covering culture, social institutions, deviance, family, religion, poverty, and crime.
- **Sports Psychology** — *Semester · Gr 9-12* — An overview of psychological theories and research relating to sport and exercise participation at both individual and group levels.

### World Languages

2027 and prior. All levels emphasize the five National World Language Standards from a biblical
perspective; levels III and above include the AAPPL proficiency test.

- **French I, II, II Honors, III, III Honors, IV Honors** — *Year* — The French sequence building communication, cultures, connections, comparisons, and communities.
- **Advanced Placement French Language and Culture** — *AP* — College-level French language and culture.
- **Latin I** — *Year* — Introduces the language of the ancient Romans through texts on daily life, family, mythology, and travel.
- **Latin II, II Honors, III, III Honors** — *Year* — The intermediate Latin sequence.
- **Latin IV Honors** — *Honors* — Reads Vulgate, Martial, Eutropius, Catullus, Ovid, and Caesar.
- **Advanced Placement Latin** — *AP* — Reads Vergil's *Aeneid* and Pliny the Younger's *Letters*.
- **Spanish I, II, II Honors, III, III Honors, IV Honors** — *Year* — The Spanish sequence building all four language skills.
- **Advanced Placement Spanish Language and Culture** — *AP* — Explores the six AP curriculum themes.
- **Advanced Placement Spanish Literature** — *AP* — A survey of Peninsular and Latin American literature, taught as an independent study course.

## Lower School (JK–Grade 4) — sources S3, S7

Published as **subject strands with grade-band content**, not named courses.

- **Bible** — *JK-4* — JK covers Heroes of the Faith, God's Love for All, and the Truth of God's Word; K a Survey of the Bible; Gr 1 Creation through the Promised Land; Gr 2 The Gospels; Gr 3 Israel; Gr 4 Acts and the Early Church plus a *Case for Christ* apologetics study, the capstone of lower school biblical learning.
- **Social Studies** — *JK-4* — JK covers friends and family, beginning maps, great Americans, and community helpers; progressing to Gr 4 geography, history and culture of North Carolina, economics, and government.
- **Science** — *JK-4* — JK covers My Five Senses, Animals, Weather and Sky, and Plants; progressing to Gr 4 Soils Rocks and Landforms, Environments, and Energy. All investigations use the scientific method and the engineering design process, blending classroom and STEM Lab work.
- **Literacy** — *JK-4* — Reading uses a structured literacy approach with phonemic awareness and phonics in early grades, shifting to morphology and active comprehension in upper grades; writing progresses from small moments and seed stories to narrative, informative, and persuasive styles via a workshop model with one-on-one conferencing.
- **Math** — *JK-4* — Emphasizes the "why" over rote memorization, building number sense and flexibility, moving from concrete manipulatives to pictorial representation to algorithmic understanding.
- **Art** — *JK-4* — An enrichment class.
- **Music** — *JK-4* — An enrichment class.
- **Media Center** — *JK-4* — An enrichment class.
- **Physical Education** — *JK-4* — An enrichment class.
- **Spanish** — *JK-4* — An enrichment class.
- **Technology** — *JK-4* — Cross-curricular projects plus keyboarding taught K–Gr 4.

**Spiritual Development:** ongoing discipleship, weekly chapels led by the spiritual life director,
and Spiritual Life and Meaning Week.

## Gaps — what Charlotte Christian does not publish

1. **No named, individually described Lower or Middle School courses.** Both division brochures
 publish subject strands and grade-band content tables only. There is no LS/MS course catalog with
 per-course titles, credits, and descriptions equivalent to the Upper School guide. Middle School
 elective names ARE published (S2 p.4) but **without any descriptions**; Lower School has no
 course-level naming at all.
2. **AP Music Theory has no catalog description.** It appears only on the guide's AP roster. Whether
 it runs, its prerequisites, and its credit value are not published.
3. **AP Spanish Literature is omitted from the AP roster** despite having a full description — the
 guide is internally inconsistent about its own AP count.
4. **Middle School Language Arts and Life Fitness have no per-grade course titles**, unlike Bible,
 Math, Science, Social Studies, and World Languages, which all have grade tables.
5. **Placement Criteria sections for Social Studies and World Languages are blank headers** in the
 guide (S1 pp. 23, 28) — criteria are stated for English, Math, and Science only.
6. **No teacher assignments, section counts, class sizes, or enrollment-per-course data** published.
7. **No AP exam pass rates or score distributions** are published; S5 only asserts students
 "routinely" earn AP Scholar awards.
8. **Winterim course list is not published** — the guide describes the program and credit structure
 but names no specific Winterim offerings.
9. **The "more than 45 AP and honors classes" figure (S5) is a marketing aggregate** and is not
 itemized anywhere; the ~21 AP + ~35 honors breakdown here was reconstructed from S1 instead.
10. **Division grade bands differ from the naive assumption.** Lower School is **JK–4** (not JK/TK–5)
 and Middle School is **5–8** (not 6–8). There is no TK at this school.
