Transparency gap — per-class National Merit Semifinalists
The school reports only rolling 15-year finalist/commended totals plus individual honorees; it does not publish a
clean per-class Semifinalist count, and a Charlotte-Christian-specific Semifinalist figure could not be verified from
public reporting. This is a reporting-format gap — the school chooses a 15-year lens rather than an annual one.
Grading and the no-rank policy, as explained to colleges
The profile states plainly that the school does not rank students. GPA is on a 4.0 scale; honors courses
receive +0.5 quality point and AP/500-level courses +1.0. All upper-school courses count toward GPA; transfer
credits are shown but excluded from the cumulative GPA. To give colleges context without a rank, the profile
publishes GPA quintiles and a full grading scale, so a reader can locate a student in the class without
head-to-head ranking. A NACAC-compliant disciplinary policy is also stated.
Transparency gap — test-optional strategy and digital portfolios
Beyond naming Scoir and the required grades 9–11 PSAT, the school does not publish a detailed written
test-optional advising strategy or a digital-portfolio (video/portfolio) program for applicants. Not published is not the
same as not practiced — it is simply not documented online.

The Honest Questions: Fit, Rank & the Whole Class
Rank policy, support beyond the top of the class, and genuine fit versus default-to-prestige.
Class rank
The school does not rank students — stated in every profile edition. To let colleges gauge rigor and standing
without pitting high achievers against one another, it substitutes GPA quintiles, a published grading scale, and
honors/AP weighting.
Support for students not at the top / non-traditional paths
The office’s stated mission is explicitly inclusive of all students — whether headed to a community college,
a gap year, the military, the workforce, or a four-year college. The Class-of-2025 enrolled roster bears this
out, including a community-college enrollee, a postgraduate program, a real-estate school, and a childcare
career path alongside four-year destinations. The Class of 2026 publicly celebrated service-academy
appointments, evidencing a documented military pathway.
Genuine fit vs. default-to-prestige
The required Junior Seminar frames “finding a college that will be a good fit,” and the counseling philosophy
defines a “good college” as one that provides opportunities for spiritual growth, allows families to be good
financial stewards, and prepares students for a career and calling — an explicitly fit-and-values framing rather
than a prestige framing. The Director has publicly cautioned against a perfectionist, must-succeed-at-everything
mindset.
Transparency gap — learning-differences support through the college process
The school publishes a robust personal/social-emotional counseling program, but it does not publish a formal
learning-support / accommodations program (e.g., a named learning-resource center) or a documented process for
securing College Board / ACT testing accommodations for students with learning differences. Families whose
students need accommodations should ask the counseling office directly. Absence of published detail is not
evidence the support does not exist.
If a student changes direction
The published philosophy — counselors “walk alongside” students, support non-four-year paths, and
emphasize fit over prestige — points to flexibility, though no formal protocol for mid-process pivots is published.
Segment confidence: ~70%
The no-rank/quintile approach and the fit-over-prestige philosophy are clearly documented, as is explicit support for
varied post-graduate paths. The one clear gap is a published learning-differences/accommodations pathway through
the college process.
Governing principle across all seven segments
Unpublished is not the same as deficient. Where this dossier flags that the school “does not publish” something,
that is a transparency gap on the school’s part — a question for the counseling office — never a verdict that the
program is weak in that area. Everything reported here reflects only what the school publishes and what public
research surfaced.

How the school’s name and documentation work on a student’s behalf with college admissions readers.

Those memberships — alongside regional accreditation (SACS/Cognia) and Christian-schools bodies (CESA)

those bodies signals to colleges that the office operates by recognized professional standards.
On-campus college-rep visits
The counseling office schedules college-representative visits through Scoir Visits and welcomes reps from
August to November, with published directions to the office. This is a rep-visit season, not an on-campus
interview program — on-campus admissions interviews are not mentioned in the published materials.

No special institutional relationships, feeder pipelines, or standing arrangements with particular colleges are
published. The profile and brochure list where students have been accepted and enrolled but assert no privileged
relationships. This is appropriate and expected — such relationships are rarely published by any school — and its
absence is not a mark against the program.
