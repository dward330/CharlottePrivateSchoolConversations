### The AP record — the school's headline academic claim

From the 2026-2027 profile (Class of 2027 cover year, 2026 exam administration):

- **179 students took 401 AP exams** in 2026. All students enrolled in AP courses are
 required to take the AP exams.
- **96% of all AP exam scores were 3 or higher**; **78% were a 4 or 5**.
- Five-year AP 3+ series (2022 → 2026): **84% · 93% · 92% · 96% · 96%** — a rising
 line the school prints as a chart.
- 2026 AP Scholar tally: **38 AP Scholars with Distinction, 27 AP Scholars with
 Honor, 33 AP Scholars**.
- Prior year for comparison (2024-2025 profile, Class of 2024): 168 students took
 344 exams; 35 Scholars with Distinction, 13 with Honor, 29 Scholars.
- 2023-24 profile (Class of 2023): 161 AP students, 324 exams, **93% scored 3+**.

### Test scores

- Class of 2026: **SAT 1263 / ACT 26** (2026-2027 profile).
- Five-year averages: **SAT 1261 / ACT 26**.
- Class of 2024: SAT 1243 / ACT 27 (2024-2025 profile).
- Class of 2023: SAT 1268 / ACT 26 (2023-24 profile).

### National Merit

Since 2006 — the year of the school's first graduating class — Covenant Day has had
**28 National Merit Finalists and 70 National Merit Commended students** (2026-2027
profile). The 2024-2025 edition said 26 and 61; the 2023-24 edition said 25 and 59,
so the ledger is still accruing.

### Rigor architecture

- Grading scale: A+ = 97-100 at **4.33 unweighted**; **Honors +0.5; Dual Enrollment
 and AP +1.0** quality point. GPA computed on all subjects taken in grades 9-12.
- **25 Carnegie Units to graduate**: 4 English, 4 Math, 3 Science, 3 Social Studies,
 3 Bible (a Bible course every year), 1 Fine Art, 0.5 Health, 1 PE, 5.5 electives
 (2026-2027 profile; the 2024-2025 edition showed 3.5 Bible / 5 electives).
- **AP pacing is deliberately gated**: no AP courses for freshmen; sophomores may
 take a maximum of one AP. "Two AP courses in the junior year and four AP courses
 senior year is a very demanding course load" (profile's own words). Placement in
 Honors/DE/AP is determined by previous coursework, grades, and teacher feedback.
- Dual enrollment through **Covenant College**; online partner **SevenStar Academy**
 (transcript notes courses taken there).
- **80% of full-time high school faculty hold advanced degrees** (2026-2027 profile;
 81% in 2024-2025; the live academics page says "over 83%").
- 37 teaching faculty for ~350 high school students (2026-2027 profile).

### Distinctive department

**Restoration & Sustainability** is a named high-school department with its own
director (Dr. Rebeca Burnett) — courses include Augustine Literacy Project,
Intercultural Practicum, Introduction to Engineering, and Restore525. The profile
gives it a stand-alone descriptive block, the only department treated that way.

### The mechanics the office owns

- **One-on-one support "through the college process, from course planning and
 building a strong application to identifying scholarship opportunities"**
 (high-school academics page).
- Academic advising on course selections tied to college goals — placement into
 Honors/DE/AP is by previous coursework, grades, and teacher feedback, so course
 planning runs through the same office that writes the school's transcript story.
- The office "meets with juniors and seniors throughout their high school careers"
 (guidance page).
- A **College Representatives** page manages visiting college admissions officers.
- The **High School Profile** itself is the office's application artifact: CEEB code
 342532, grading scale, rank policy, AP gating, and the acceptance list in one
 two-page PDF refreshed annually — three editions verified.
- Transcript policy: grades/GPAs on the CDS transcript reflect courses taken at CDS
 or the online partner SevenStar Academy, as noted; transcripts from other schools
 attended are attached as separate documents.

### The rank story an application rides on

The profile states the no-rank policy in full: "Due to the small class size and the
college-bound nature of Covenant Day's population, we believe that class rank is not
a strong discriminator from which to predict college performance. Therefore, we do
not rank." Colleges instead get the 4.33/+0.5/+1.0 weighting key and the AP-gating
rigor statement, letting readers reconstruct rigor-in-context.

### Not published

No essay-workshop calendar, application-boot-camp, or interview-prep programming is
named publicly; no application platform (Common App workflow, Naviance/SCOIR) is
named. The support model as published is relational — a 30-year director, small
class, junior/senior meeting cadence — rather than a productized curriculum.

### Fit philosophy

The office's stated aim is fit, in so many words: "match each student with the
college or university that best suits his or her interests and talents." The
matriculation footprint bears it out — the bold (enrolled) institutions on the
2023-2026 list run from Dartmouth and Duke through Covenant College, Cedarville,
and community colleges, with the deepest concentrations at NC publics (Chapel
Hill, NC State, UNC Charlotte, App State, ECU) and Christian colleges.

### What the whole-class picture shows — and what it withholds

The school publishes strong **aggregate** tables and **no distributions**:

- Five-year AP 3+ series: 84 → 93 → 92 → 96 → 96% (2022-2026).
- Class SAT/ACT averages per class (1263/26 for 2026; 1243/27 for 2024; 1268/26
 for 2023) and five-year averages (1261/26).
- AP Scholar tiers per class (38 Distinction / 27 Honor / 33 Scholars in 2026).
- National Merit cumulative ledger (28 Finalists, 70 Commended since 2006).

**No GPA distribution, no score quartiles/quintiles, no per-class matriculation
rates** appear in any of the three profile editions. This follows directly from the
school's stated no-rank policy ("we do not rank") — a school that declines to rank
does not publish distribution tables either. Confirmed consistent across all three
editions; a deeper pass will not recover it.

### Support for the middle and below

- 5 Learning Specialists and 1 Academic Resources Director on the high school
 staff table (2026-2027 profile; the 2024-2025 edition listed 5 Academic Resources
 Specialists + 1 Academic Resources Director + 1 Library Media Specialist).
- A Clinical Counselor (Emily Lee, LMFT) distinct from the college office.
- The tuition terms note tutoring/special testing beyond classroom help is
 available at extra cost — a disclosed, priced support channel.
- AP gating (none for freshmen, one for sophomores) is itself a whole-class
 guardrail: rigor is sequenced rather than open-enrollment.

- **Restoration & Sustainability** — a named high-school department (unique among
 the schools in this project) with its own director and course ladder (Augustine
 Literacy Project, Intercultural Practicum, Introduction to Engineering,
 Restore525). The profile gives it a stand-alone block: students "dive deeply into
 facets of our community, society, and world in a manner that develops
 problem-solving skills, leadership, and practical abilities, through a biblical
 worldview." A transcript line no other Charlotte-area applicant carries.
- **Senior Capstone Project** — listed among the high school's "meaningful
 milestones."
- **ContainIt** — high-school students converting shipping containers into housing
 for the Pine Ridge Reservation (South Dakota), aiming to house "more than 30
 teens" — a concrete, multi-year service build with the students' own trades work
 in it.
- **Dual-enrollment + AP layering** — Covenant College DE credit on Honors
 Calculus/Statistics/Literature & Philosophy lets a transcript carry college credit
 beyond the 15-course AP matrix.
- **The rigor narrative** — AP gating plus the required-exam policy (every AP
 student sits the exam; 96% score 3+) makes the school's AP grades legible to
 admissions readers in a way open-enrollment AP programs are not.
- **Mock Trial + courtroom-artist results** — Cornelia Knight (Covenant Day School
 Blue team) won the NC Mock Trial courtroom-artist regional (Hickory) and was 2025
 **state runner-up** — see the Student Clubs research for sourcing.

### The honest scope

These are differentiation-by-depth stories (service builds, a bespoke department,
denominational college credit) rather than national-competition credentials; the
school publishes no research-program, Intel/Regeneron, or Olympiad results.

## Derived figures

The five-year figure is **pooled** — `sum(admitted) / sum(applied)` across the five
most recent terms — and deliberately **not** the mean of the five annual rates, which
would weight a 6-applicant year the same as a 60-applicant one.

## Interpretation cautions

- **This is not the university's admit rate, and not the school's selectivity.** It is
 the rate at which that university admitted *this high school's* applicants — a joint
 property of the pair. Label it that way wherever it is surfaced.
- **Carry the denominator.** Several cells here are single-digit; a 100% rate off 2
 applicants is not comparable to one off 200, and a bare percentage from a
 single-digit base must not be published.
- **UNC-system campuses only.** This says nothing about private or out-of-state
 destinations, so it complements a matriculation list and never replaces one.

## Sanity checks run

`Admitted ≤ Applied` and `Enrolled ≤ Admitted` in every published term; every displayed
Admit Rate re-derived from that term's own Applied/Admitted to within rounding. The
scrape was smoke-tested first against the method file's reference table (Cannon School
× UNC-Chapel Hill: Fall 2016 = 44 applied / 26 admitted / 13 enrolled / 59.1%; Fall
2025 = 63 applied / 20 admitted / 31.7%), which reproduced exactly.
