## 1e. Verdict

### Honest reasons this program may fit a family
1. **A real, non-auditioned entry point into performing music.** Contemporary Ensemble is
 explicitly "open to students at any level... no audition required," repertoire is "adjusted
 to the level of each student," and instruments are available in the classroom. For a
 beginner or a late starter, that is a materially lower barrier than an auditioned band or
 choir program. [VERIFIED]
2. **Small-school math means real stage time.** Davidson Day mounts a full Upper School
 musical (*Big Fish*, *Once Upon a One More Time*, *Mean Girls*) out of a very small student
 body. A student who wants to be cast is competing against far fewer peers than at a large
 independent or public magnet.
3. **The Blumey record is legitimate and program-level, not just lucky individuals.** The 2023
 *Mean Girls* nominations included **Best Ensemble/Chorus** and **Best Overall Direction**,
 and 2026 *Big Fish* produced **three finalists** across Best Actor, Best Supporting
 Actress, and Best Featured Performer. Small program, real competitive results. [VERIFIED]
4. **A genuinely complete visual-arts ladder terminating in AP.** Photography I-II-III and
 Studio I → Studio II Honors → AP Studio Art is a full four-to-five-year runway, and
 Portfolio offers a second, more self-directed route in. For a small school, that is more
 depth than the enrollment would predict. [VERIFIED]
5. **Digital/production breadth beyond the traditional arts.** Film Studies & Production and
 Modern Music (GarageBand composition, soundtrack building) plus Photography III's
 personal-website requirement give a media-leaning student real, portfolio-visible output
 without a performance requirement. [VERIFIED]
6. **The requirements are arts-friendly at the margins.** Only **1 fine-arts credit** is
 required to graduate, so an arts-indifferent student is not forced into a heavy commitment —
 while an arts-committed one can **claim the PE credit for theater participation**, freeing
 a schedule slot. Small but real. [VERIFIED — catalog p. 8]

**Fairness caveat parents should hear plainly:** this is an *accessible* arts program, not a
*conservatory* one. There is one performing music ensemble and it is a rock band; there is no
band, orchestra, or chorus course; there is no stagecraft course; no arts facility is named;
no music-honors or art-honor-society pipeline is documented; and no arts faculty member is
publicly named. Several of those may exist and simply not be published — which is exactly why
the tour questions below matter more here than at a school that publishes everything.

### Tour questions — weighted toward the unverifiable
1. **"Where do performances actually happen?"** No arts venue is named anywhere, and catalog
 photos appear to show a musical staged in the gym and a recital held inside the art
 studio. Ask to physically stand in the performance space. Is there a dedicated
 theater/auditorium with fixed seating, stage lighting, and a booth — or is it a gym or
 multipurpose room that gets converted? What is the seat count?
2. **"Who teaches the arts, and who directs the musical?"** Not one arts faculty name is
 published. Ask for names, credentials, tenure at the school, and specifically who directed
 *Big Fish* — and whether that person is returning. In a program this small, one departure
 can change everything.
3. **"Is there any concert band, orchestra, or chorus — in any division, at any level?"** The
 Upper School catalog shows only Contemporary Ensemble. If a child plays violin, flute, or
 sings classically, ask directly what they would join in grades 9-12, whether a Middle
 School band or chorus exists and feeds anywhere, and whether private lessons happen on
 campus.
4. **"Who builds the sets, and is there any tech-theater pathway?"** The productions clearly
 involve substantial constructed scenery, and a Davidson Day student once won a scholarship
 for non-performing theater work, yet there is no stagecraft course. Ask whether there is a
 student crew, who supervises it, and whether a design/tech-focused student has a real path.
5. **"What external arts recognition have students earned in the last three years — beyond
 the Blumeys?"** No all-district/all-state music selections, no adjudicated ratings, no
 NCTC participation, no National Art Honor Society chapter, and no Tri-M chapter could be
 found. Ask whether any of these exist and, if not, whether the school will support a
 student who wants to pursue them independently.
6. **"How does visual art get shown, and is there any darkroom or film photography?"** No
 gallery space, student-show calendar, or art honor society is published, and the entire
 photo sequence reads as digital/Lightroom-only. Ask where student work is exhibited, how
 often, whether AP Studio Art portfolios get a public show, whether the school provides
 cameras, and whether film/analog work is possible at all.
