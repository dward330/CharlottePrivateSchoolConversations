 Middle, and Upper School. **[VERIFIED]**

**Lower School (TK–4, with 5th grade ensembles)**

 **[VERIFIED]** https://www.providenceday.org/lower-school/arts

### Where PD actually sits in the Blumey field — honest assessment

- The field is large and getting larger: **59 schools** submitted productions for the 2026
 Blumeys, up from **54** the prior year. **[VERIFIED]**
 https://www.blumenthalarts.org/news/detail/blumenthal-arts-announces-nominees-finalists-for-2026-blumey-awards
- **Providence Day is a consistent participant and a reliable producer of individual acting
 finalists — but it is not a Blumey powerhouse.** In the two most recent cycles (2025 *Peter
 Pan*, 2026 *Sweet Charity*) it placed 5–6 students as **finalists** each year and converted
 **zero** into wins, and in neither year did it appear in any production-level category
 (Best Musical, Best Ensemble, Best Direction, Best Choreography, Best Student Orchestra).
 **[VERIFIED]** (2025 PDF + 2026 winners page)
- **Northwest School of the Arts**, by direct contrast, took **three 2026 wins** — Best
 Supporting Actor (Wiley Seago), Best Supporting Actress (Andie Jones), and Best Student Stage
 Manager (Spencer Angel), all for *Head Over Heels* — off seven nominations. **CATA** took
 **Best Student Orchestra** for *Pippin (2013 Revival)* off five nominations. **[VERIFIED]**
 https://www.blumenthalarts.org/news/detail/blumenthal-arts-announces-2026-blumey-award-winners
- The 2026 top prizes went to **Weddington** (Best Musical Tier 1, *Legally Blonde*) and
 **North Lincoln** (Best Musical Tier 2, *Hadestown Teen Edition*), and the two **Jimmy Awards**
 representatives were from **North Lincoln** and **Carmel Christian** — none from PD.
 **[VERIFIED]** (same URL)
- Fair summary for parents: **PD's high-water mark was 2016–2022** (a Best Musical nomination in
 2016, and in 2022 a Best Musical Tier-2 + Best Direction nomination with one outright win).
 Its recent seasons are strong on **individual performer development** and **student design**
 but have not been converting at the production level. Notably, the one category where PD keeps
 showing up on the *design* side is **Best Student Set Designer** (April Liu, 2024 and 2026) —
 consistent with the program's evident investment in set construction.

## 1e — Verdict

### Why the program holds up — 3–5 concrete reasons

1. **The facility is real and central, not an afterthought.** The **McMahon Fine Arts Center**
 is a **37,000 sq ft, 1990-built complex with a 500-seat theater** plus dedicated music and
 art classrooms — and its **foyer** doubles as a gallery. Every division performs in the same
 theatre, so a 3rd grader sees the room she'll perform in as a senior.
 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Day_School ;
 https://www.providenceday.org/the-pd-difference/get-involved/friends-of-the-arts)

2. **The NCTC one-act record is genuinely strong and recent — stronger than the Blumey record.**
 Between 2019 and 2024 PD collected **Superior Ratings**, an **Overall Regional Site Winner
 (Adjudicators' Choice)**, a **Regional Site Winner Audience Choice**, **Excellence in
 Directing twice**, and **advanced to the State Festival** with *Radium Girls*, winning
 **state-level** Lighting and Set Design honors. This is a competitive theatre program that
 wins, just in a different arena than the Blumeys.
 (https://www.providenceday.org/performances-and-awards)

3. **The honors-music pipeline is calendared infrastructure, not aspiration.** NCAIS Honors Band
 (UNC Charlotte), NCAIS Honors Chorus (Gardner-Webb), NC-ASTA All-State Orchestra Clinic, Mars
 Hill Choral Clinic, NC Honors Chorus Weekend, All-District Band auditions and a **Tri-M
 induction** all sit as fixed dates on the published arts calendar. Backed by a hard, verified
 result: **five students into the 2026 ACDA Southern Region Honor Choir**, chosen from **1,500+
 applicants across 11 states**. (https://www.providenceday.org/upper-school/arts ; ACDA news post)

4. **Technical theatre and design are treated as first-class disciplines.** Tech theatre starts
 in **Middle School**; the awards page gives **"Set Builds"** its own media section; students
 win **named NCTC awards in Lighting Design, Set Design, Stage Management and Set Carpentry**;
 and **April Liu** earned **Blumey Best Student Set Designer** placements in both **2024 and
 2026**. A kid who'd rather build than sing has a real, credentialed path here.

5. **A working wet darkroom.** "Developing film in the darkroom" is explicitly part of the US
 visual arts offering — an increasingly uncommon facility that signals real studio investment,
 and it's corroborated by a student **Scholastic Gold Key in photography**.
 (https://www.providenceday.org/upper-school/arts)

**The honest counterweight (state this plainly to parents):** in the **Blumey** arena — the
region's highest-profile high-school musical theatre competition, now **59 schools** — Providence
Day has produced **many finalists but, verifiably, one win** (Max Odom, Best Supporting Actor,
*Xanadu*, 2022). In both **2025** and **2026** it placed 5–6 acting/design finalists and
converted **none**, and appeared in **no** production-level category. Northwest School of the
Arts took **three wins** in 2026 alone; CATA took Best Student Orchestra. PD is a strong
*participant* and a good developer of individual performers — it is not currently in the top
tier of the Charlotte musical-theatre field.

### 4–6 sharp questions to ask on a campus tour

1. **"Show me the visual arts course sequence on paper — what exactly does a student take
 between Middle School art and AP Studio Art?"** The website describes a progressive ladder but
 **never names the intermediate courses**. Ask for the actual course catalog, prerequisites,
 and how many sections of each run. Also ask directly: **is there ceramics, and is there a
 kiln?** — neither is mentioned anywhere on the site.

2. **"Of the '26 Blumey Awards' on your awards page, how many were outright wins versus
 nominations or finalist placements?"** The page lists them undifferentiated; independent
 cross-checking supports **one** verified win. A confident, specific answer tells you how
 straight the program is with families.

3. **"In 2025 and 2026 you had five and six Blumey finalists and no wins, and no production-level
 nominations. What's the plan to convert?"** Ask specifically about **Best Musical, Best
 Ensemble, Best Direction, and Best Student Orchestra** — the categories PD has been absent
 from — and whether they staff a choreographer and a pit orchestra for the spring musical.

4. **"How many students actually make honors ensembles each year, and out of how many who
 audition?"** The "over 100 musicians" and "over 50 superior ratings" figures carry **no time
 window** and are almost certainly cumulative. Ask for **last year's** numbers: how many
 auditioned for All-District/NCAIS/All-State, and how many were seated.

5. **"Can a student do a sport and a spring musical?"** The published musical/play calendar is
 dense — fall one-act in October, NCTC festivals in late Oct/Nov, musical auditions in
 December, performances in April — and PD is a strong athletics school. Ask how conflicts are
 actually adjudicated, and whether arts ensembles compete with athletics for the same block.

6. **"Is the Jazz Ensemble and ChargHers auditioned, and when does a student first get to
 audition for something?"** Ask what happens to the kid who doesn't make ChargHers — is there a
 non-auditioned choral home for them through senior year? Also worth asking who currently
 directs the Upper School musical, since **Caroline Bower** (director of the 2015–2017
 award-winning run) is no longer on the roster and **Brooke Fulton** now leads.

Program Emphasis
PD's Visual Arts courses emphasize fundamental processes while pushing students to be "original,
resourceful, and innovative." Students work across studio media - from traditional drawing and painting
to darkroom film photography and unconventional materials - developing the skills, discipline, and
self-confidence of practicing artists.
Advanced & AP Study
Upper School students can pursue AP Studio Art and AP Art History. The annual AP Art Show
showcases portfolio work, which the school frames around creativity, reflection, and connection. A
sequence of studio courses (e.g., Art I / Art II and beyond) builds toward these advanced offerings.
