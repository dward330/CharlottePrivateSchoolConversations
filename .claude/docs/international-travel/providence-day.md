## TL;DR

- **What exists [Official]:** One TK–12 Global Education program framed as "Inward.
 Outward. Forward." Lower School learning is virtual and classroom-based. Middle School
 offers student travel, Round Square conferences and a "summer PDS trip." Upper School
 offers faculty-led trips, Round Square conferences, exchanges and Big Builds, plus the
 optional Global Studies Diploma (grades 9–12). Between 2021–22 and 2024–25, the school's
 Global Map logs student travel to roughly 19 countries or territories outside the U.S.
 (our count).
- **What's missing:** No official page gives the starting grade for international
 travel. Middle School trips are documented, but the earliest eligible grade is never
 stated. Trip costs, application rules, chaperone ratios and safety policies are also
 unpublished. The 2026–27 calendar lists "Global Trip Registration Opens" and a virtual
 Global Trips information session, but trip details are only given out through registration.
- **Money:** One endowment specifically funds annual student travel to International
 Academy Amman in Jordan: the **Daher Family Round Square/Jordan Endowment**, not the
 Bessant/Clay Endowment. The Bessant/Clay Endowment funds a Global Scholar award and the
 Global Education Department. Two "Financial Assistance Beyond Tuition" funds cover
 "class trips" for students with financial need, but none explicitly mentions global
 trips. Ask the school.

## 1. Program overview & structure

**Names and framework [Official].** The school calls its program **Global Education** and
describes it as "woven throughout the student experience from Transitional Kindergarten
through graduation." It is organized around three stages: **INWARD** (self-awareness),
**OUTWARD** (engaging with diverse cultures) and **FORWARD** (applying learning to
"contribute to a changing world"). Each division has its own theme:

- Lower School: "Wonder + Awareness"
- Middle School: "Exploration + Connection"
- Upper School: "Leadership + Impact"

The overall TK–12 global framework is branded the **"PD Passport,"** which the school says
"infuses traditional classes with the content, skills, and character dispositions critical
to becoming a global citizen." (roundsquare + 2)

**Mission framing [Official].** The school says it intentionally creates "experiences that
foster intercultural learning inside and outside the classroom," aiming for students to
become "empathetic, informed, and empowered global citizens." The school-wide mission is to
"inspire in its students a passion for learning, a commitment to personal integrity, and a
sense of social responsibility." The Strategic Framework describes Providence Day as "one of
just six schools nationwide to offer a Global Studies Diploma and a Round Square school."

**Office and leadership (titles only) [Official].** An earlier version of the Upper School
Global Education page listed: Executive Director of Global Education; Assistant Director of
Global Programs; Director of Global Studies Diploma. Older news posts refer to a "Global
Education Director" and a "Global Education Office."

**Memberships [Official].**
- **Round Square:** Global Member. The school describes the network as "280+ like-minded
 schools in 50 countries on six continents."
- **Institute for Global Learning and Global Connections:** the school's Global Education
 page says PD "is a member of the Institute for Global Learning and Global Connections"
 (the Institute for Global Learning was formerly the Global Education Benchmark Group).
- **Global Online Academy:** listed on the Upper School page.

**History (key dates) [Official].**
- 2005 or 2007: first Global Studies Diploma graduates. Official sources conflict (§5).
- 2006: the faculty Global Educator Certificate begins.
- 2016: the school becomes a Round Square Global Member.

**Scale metrics.**
- Students abroad per year and trips per year: not published.
- Group sizes on the Global Map [Official]: Upper School — 10 students to Scandinavia, 12
 to South Korea, and 6 each to Round Square conferences. Middle School — 10 to the
 Galápagos and 6 to a Round Square conference in England.
- Graduates per year: the GSD program site says the school "typically graduates an
 average of 20 seniors per year" with the diploma. (google)

## 2. Grade-level eligibility

- **Exact starting grade: NOT PUBLISHED.** No official page names the first grade
 eligible for international travel.
- **Lower School (TK–5) [Official]:** No student travel was found. Global learning
 includes:
 - "TK Goes Global," covering China, Germany, India and Kenya, where students "fly"
 between classrooms with passports.
 - A project on a different world region in each grade from TK through 5.
 - Third graders in Round Square "Worldwide Show & Tell."
 - Kindergarten video calls with Collingwood School (Canada).
 - A Lower School Spanish video exchange with Markham College (Peru).
 - The Round Square "Heroes of Discovery" framework for grades K–8.
- **Middle School [Official]:** The Middle School page says students "may take advantage
 of opportunities for world travel, including a Middle School conference with Round
 Square and a summer PDS trip." Documented Middle School trips:
 - Greece (summer 2024)
 - Galápagos, Ecuador (2024–25)
 - A Round Square conference at Milton Abbey School, England (2024–25)
 - A Round Square conference in Atlanta, GA (2023–24, domestic)
 - Hawaii (domestic, year not stated)
 - Earlier domestic trips to Arizona and California (2021–22)

 The specific Middle School grades eligible are not stated.
- **Upper School (9–12) [Official]:** Faculty-led educational trips, Round Square
 international conferences, exchanges, Big Builds and service projects. (roundsquare)
 The admissions page describes "faculty-led spring break and summer travel options."
- **Can 8th graders start the GSD?** Official sources describe the GSD as a grades 9–12
 program; one says "Starting in ninth grade." The GSD program site says students
 "typically begin their GSD journey in 9th grade" and "can join later in Upper School
 under certain circumstances." (google) **No source says 8th graders can start.**

## 5. Global Studies Diploma (GSD)

- **What it is [Official]:** "an optional second diploma that recognizes students who
 pursue advanced global learning throughout their Upper School experience." Official copy
 says Providence Day was "the first in the nation."
- **Grade span:** grades 9–12.
- **Required courses [Official, GSD program site hosted on the school's Google domain]:**
 Global Civics; World History (or AP World History); Comparative Government and
 Politics; 4 years of the same world language; Two Global Studies courses; Global
 Leadership; 2.5 GSD elective credits; a minimum grade of B- in all required GSD courses.
- **Co-curricular requirements [Official]:** "Traveling abroad and/or hosting exchange
 students"; attending local global speaker events; presenting global experiences to
 peers; taking civic action.
- **How travel counts:** Older catalogs say students who "travel abroad, host an
 international student, or attend global events may be able to earn global points"
 (2017–18 catalog). The 2014–15 catalog required 750 points for extracurricular and
 cross-cultural experiences. The current GSD site describes a student portfolio. It also
 mentions an optional "Global Ambassador" distinction, created in 2021. Treat the points
 system as **historical** until the school confirms otherwise.
- **Capstone [Official]:** The Global Leadership course is required for seniors. A 2018–19
 school profile describes "a lengthy global research paper in the capstone Global
 Leadership course." The current site describes an individual portfolio.
- **Graduates (official figures conflict):**
 - The admissions page says "Since 2007, more than 250 students."
 - The GSD program site says the program "started in 2005 with 5 students" and "more
 than 325" have graduated. The program alumni page says "over 300."
 - One commencement article recorded 27 GSD recipients in a single class. That article is
 undated, so treat it as historical.
- **Recognition tied to the GSD [Official]:** the Providence Day Distinguished Global
 Scholar Award goes to one GSD candidate each year. Recipients must maintain a GPA of 3.75
 or higher. The award is funded by the Bessant/Clay Endowment.

## 8. Cost & financial aid

- **Per-trip costs: NOT PUBLISHED** anywhere on the official site.
- **Aid that may apply [Official, Endowment page, published August 27, 2026]:**
 - **Gilmer L. Murdock, Jr. Endowment Fund (2007):** awards cover "'extra' expenses
 including tutoring, SAT review classes, calculators, class trips, sports equipment,
 team camps, and special tournaments for any student demonstrating financial need."
 - **Latchwood Endowment (2020–21):** helps cover "extra school-related costs, including
 tutoring, class trips, Dining Hall expenses, and fees for extracurricular activities."
 - **Bhandari Mehta Endowment (2020):** partly funds "financial assistance to students
 who have a demonstrated need beyond tuition."
 - **Interpretation:** "class trips" is the closest wording. None of these funds
 explicitly mentions international or global trips, so global trips may or may not
 qualify.
- **Travel-specific fund [Official]:** The **Daher Family Round Square/Jordan Endowment**
 "supports annual travel opportunities for Providence Day students to travel to the
 International Academy of Amman (IAA) in Jordan, a fellow Round Square member school." In
 years with no student recipient, the fund may support faculty travel.
- **Correction to a common assumption:** The **Bessant/Clay Endowment for Global
 Education** (2018) does **not** fund the Jordan trip. Per the Endowment page, it supports
 the Distinguished Global Scholar Award and "expenses related to the Global Education
 Department." Whether department funds subsidize student trips is not stated.
- **Faculty travel:** The Desoer Family Global Studies Faculty Award (2008) funds Upper
 School faculty travel abroad. It is not student aid.

## 11. Gaps & questions to ask the school

1. **Starting grade:** What is the earliest grade allowed on an international trip? Are
 Middle School trips open to 6th graders, or only to 7th–8th graders?
2. **Current trip list:** Which trips are offered for 2026–27 and summer 2027? What are the
 dates, durations and grade bands?
3. **Cost:** What is the price range per trip, and what does it include (airfare,
 insurance, meals)? What are the deposit and refund policies?
4. **Aid:** Do the Murdock, Latchwood or Bhandari Mehta "beyond tuition" funds cover
 global trips? How does a family apply, and when?
5. **Jordan/IAA:** How are students selected for the Daher-funded trip? Which grades are
 eligible, when does it happen, and is it fully funded?
6. **Selection:** Are trips first-come, first-served or application-based? How are Round
 Square delegates chosen?
7. **Safety:** What are the chaperone ratios, travel vendor(s), insurance, emergency
 protocols, and passport and visa timelines?
8. **Exchanges:** How do families apply for Round Square exchanges? Are host families
 required to host in return?
9. **GSD:** Is the points system still used? Does one trip satisfy the travel
 requirement? Is there a GSD-specific travel subsidy?
10. **Recognition:** What was the July 2026 "national recognition," and which organization
 gave it?
11. **Scale:** How many students travel abroad each year, and what share of each grade
 does that represent?

(WebSearch / WebFetch / Wayback Machine). It checked each claim in the AI-generated

app only if a row here verifies it.

**Verification levels**
- **fetched**: page text retrieved and the claim read in it.
- **live, tile**: text sits in a click-to-open tile, retrieved from
 `https://www.providenceday.org/fs/elements/<el>?is_popup=true&post_id=<id>&show_post=true`.
 Cite the parent page URL.
- **Wayback**: the claim is gone from the live page but present in a 2026-04-21 snapshot.
 The school redesigned these pages in summer or fall 2026.
- **snippet only**: seen only in a search-result snippet; the page itself 404'd.
 **Not sufficient to render.** Re-fetch at implement time, or drop the claim.

older snapshot disagree, **the live page wins**.
