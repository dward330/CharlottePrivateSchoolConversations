How to read this report
What this report covers
This report sets out what Charlotte Christian School publishes about the cost of attending, and about the
tuition assistance it offers to families of students in junior kindergarten through grade twelve. It reports
published figures, names the document each figure came from, and states plainly where a figure is not
published.
This is a K–12 report. Tuition assistance here means the school's own need-based grants that reduce a
family's K–12 tuition bill. It is a different subject from college financial aid — the FAFSA, the CSS Profile,
college merit awards and scholarships won by graduating seniors. The school publishes figures in that
second category as well, and they are deliberately excluded here. Mixing the two produces a badly
misleading picture of what a family pays, so this report keeps them apart.
Two rules this report follows
An unpublished figure is a transparency gap, not a verdict on the school. Where the school does not
put a number on the public record, this report says so and moves on. It does not treat silence as evidence
of a weak or ungenerous programme, and no reader should. Independent schools differ widely in what they
choose to disclose, and disclosure practice is not programme quality.
Tuition and fees change every year. Every figure below carries the school year it applies to. Rates are
announced annually and the figures in this report will be superseded. Before making any financial decision,
confirm current numbers directly with the school's business office.
What this report will not do
It does not estimate what any particular family would pay, does not net tuition against an award, and does
not judge whether the cost is high or low. Those are judgements for a family to make with its own numbers.
This report supplies the published inputs and identifies what is missing.

A closing note on what this report is
Charlotte Christian publishes its prices clearly and its assistance process in real detail. It publishes very little
about what assistance actually reaches families. Both of those things are true at once, and neither is a
verdict on the school. A family should read the published detail for what it is worth, take the unanswered
questions to the business office, and make its own decision with its own numbers.
Figures change annually. Confirm anything that matters directly with the school before acting on it. This report is not financial
advice.
