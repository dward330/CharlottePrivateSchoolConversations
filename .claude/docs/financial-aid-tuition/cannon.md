Before You Read This
What this report covers
This report sets out what Cannon School publishes about the cost of attending: tuition at every price band the school
publishes, the fees and costs that sit on top of tuition, how the school's need-based financial aid process works, what
the school discloses about the size and reach of that aid, the payment plans available, and the multi-year picture. Every
figure carries the school year or fiscal year it applies to, and every figure is traceable to a named source in the list at the
end.
Two kinds of aid that are easy to confuse
This report is about K–12 tuition assistance — the grants Cannon School itself awards to reduce a family's tuition
bill while the student is enrolled at Cannon. It is not about college financial aid: the FAFSA, the CSS Profile, college
merit awards and college award-letter comparison are a different process, at a different stage of life, run by different
institutions. Cannon's own website uses the phrase “financial aid” for the K–12 grants described here. Where you see it
in this report, it means the K–12 grant.
Unpublished does not mean deficient
A number of the questions this report asks end in “the school does not publish this.” That is a statement about what is
on the public record and nothing more. It is not a finding that the school is weak, secretive, or falling short. Schools
make different choices about what to put on a public webpage versus what to discuss with a family directly, and many
figures that are absent from a website are readily supplied by a business office on request. Every gap in this report is
flagged in a slate-blue box, and the final section gathers them into a list of questions a family can ask directly.
Published does not mean current
Tuition and fees reset every year, and retired pages linger in search results long after the school has replaced them.
For that reason every dollar figure and every percentage in this report is tagged with the year it applies to. Where a
figure could not be tied to a confirmed year, it was left out rather than printed without one.
What this report does not do
(cid:127) It does not judge whether the price is high or low. It reports the number, the year, and what the number does and
does not include.
(cid:127) It does not calculate what any family would pay. Tuition minus an average grant is not a price anyone is charged,
and no such figure appears here.
(cid:127) It does not advise any family on whether to apply for aid, or on how to plan for the cost.
(cid:127) It does not name any student, family, donor, staff member or award recipient. Financial aid information is reported
in totals only.

The single most notable thing about how Cannon publishes cost information
Cannon discloses an unusually complete financial aid picture, but it is split across separate documents that carry
different years, and the largest figure is not on the financial aid page. The grant ranges and the maximum-grant rule sit

the financial aid page from top to bottom would never encounter the total. This is an observation about where
information is placed, not about how much aid the school gives.

The deposit
A non-refundable tuition deposit of $2,000 is required on acceptance for 2026–27 and is applied toward the total tuition.
Families applying for financial aid pay a reduced non-refundable deposit of $650, also applied toward tuition. Because

Whether tuition differs for new versus returning students
The school publishes one rate per band and does not distinguish between new and returning students. Cannon is a day

The all-in annual cost estimate: why this report does not print one
This series normally assembles published line items into a floor and a common range per division, so that families have
a single figure the school itself does not publish. That estimate is built only where at least two-thirds of the components
can be priced from published sources, because a range assembled from mostly-missing parts misleads more than it
helps.
For a Grade 9 student in 2026–27, eleven cost components can be confirmed to genuinely exist — seven of them from
the fee document and bus tariff, and four more confirmed by the financial aid pages, which name them among the costs
a grant will not cover. Seven of the eleven are priced: tuition, the required class trip, books, the required device,
Winterm, Advanced Placement exams and bus service. Four are not: an annual dining figure, athletic team packs and
equipment, arts and music supplies and instrument rental, and the Tuition Refund Plan. That is roughly 36% of
components unpriced, which is over the threshold, so no estimate is printed here. It is a narrow miss, and the four
missing items are exactly the four to ask the business office about. For the Lower School the question does not arise,
because no Lower School costs are priced at all.

How an application is assessed
Cannon's grants are strictly need-based. A family files through Clarity Tuition, a third-party platform, which reviews
income, assets, liabilities and family size and returns a recommended family contribution. The school's financial aid
committee then reviews that recommendation and sets the final grant. The committee is composed by role rather than
by name in the school's published material: the director of financial aid, the director of business and finance, and an
associate director of admission.
Whether applying for aid affects the admission decision
The school states directly that admission decisions are made completely separately from financial aid applications, and
that applying for aid does not influence a student's admission decision in any way. Cannon does not use the terms
“need-blind” or “need-aware” on its published pages, so this report reports the school's own wording rather than
assigning it a label.
Renewal
Grants are determined annually and families must reapply each year through Clarity so that awards reflect any change
in circumstances. The school does not publish any statement about whether awards typically renew at a consistent
level for a family whose circumstances have not changed.
Documentation, and the rule for separated and divorced parents
Tax returns and forms are required of all parents. Cannon states that it expects parents to contribute to their children's
educational costs to the full extent they are able, and that this applies equally to parents who are separated or divorced:
both the custodial and the non-custodial parent must each complete a Clarity application. The school further
states that remarriage creates a new family unit, and that the income and assets of the entire unit — including
step-parents — are treated as relevant to calculating need, though the obligations of a parent to a new family situation
are considered. Cannon states it will not allocate a grant proportionally between two households.
Confidentiality
The school states its aid programme is administered in the strictest confidence and asks recipients and their families to
keep grant information confidential as well.
Repayment
Aid at Cannon is a grant, not a loan. The school states it is not owed back to the school.

PUBLICATION GAP — Three process questions are not answered publicly
When aid decisions are released. The school publishes an aid deadline of 15 February 2026 and, separately, an
admission timeline in which applications received by 15 January receive first-round decisions on 1 March. It does not publish
when a financial aid decision reaches a family, or whether it arrives before, with, or after the admission decision — which
matters to a family deciding whether it can accept a place.
Appeals and reconsideration. No process is published for a family that wishes to ask the committee to revisit an award.
Mid-year changes. No policy is published for a family whose financial circumstances change after an award has been set
— a job loss partway through the year, for instance.

Both Student Profile editions say the aid went to a share “of students” without saying which students. The same panel of

choosing a denominator the school has not specified, and would turn a rounded percentage into a false headcount.

The school states plainly that no financial aid grant covers the full cost of tuition, and that families receiving the
maximum grant are required to pay $3,000 per year per student toward tuition. It notes separately that some fees are
not covered by grants at all.
Two published statements on the same page do not sit easily together, and this report reports the discrepancy rather
than choosing between them. The published grant range for Grades 9–12 tops out at $26,000, while published Grades

Merit awards: an answer, not a gap
On two separate pages Cannon states plainly that it does not offer merit scholarships, and specifically that it offers no
academic, arts or athletics scholarships, directing families instead to its need-based programme. This is a published
answer rather than an omission, and it means the questions this series usually asks about merit-award size, criteria
and volume do not apply here.
Discounts
Cannon publishes sibling pricing for bus service — $1,890 rather than $1,990 for a round-trip sibling in 2026–27,
and $1,200 rather than $1,270 one-way. That is a transport discount, and it should not be read as a tuition discount: no
sibling discount on tuition is published. Nor is any employee tuition remission, clergy discount, military discount or
first-responder discount. The school also publishes no indexed or variable tuition programme.
PUBLICATION GAP — Whether other discounts exist at all is unclear
For sibling tuition, employee remission, and clergy, military or first-responder discounts, the school's pages are silent rather
than negative. Silence is not the same as a stated “no” — compare the merit-scholarship question above, where Cannon
does state a clear no. A family for whom one of these would apply cannot tell from the public record whether the discount
does not exist or simply is not advertised, and should ask.
Donor-supported special funds
Alongside the main aid programme, Cannon publishes a set of donor-supported special funds for families who could
benefit from support beyond a financial aid grant. Each fund has its own criteria and families apply through a request
form. The school also maintains endowed funds that support students, including at least one endowed scholarship fund
and endowments supporting athletics, science, service and travel, and awards for graduating students pursuing
particular fields. Several of these funds are named for individuals; this report describes them by purpose rather than by
name, consistent with its rule of not naming individuals. Whether a special fund can be held alongside a need-based
grant is not published.
The North Carolina Opportunity Scholarship, and Cannon's exit
The North Carolina Opportunity Scholarship is a state programme that pays part of a private school's tuition and fees,
administered by the state education assistance authority rather than by any school. Cannon has participated, and has
announced that it will conclude participation after the 2025–26 school year. The school's stated reason is that ending
participation allows it to continue designing curriculum and programmes shaped by its own mission and values; it
directs families seeking tuition support to its need-based programme instead.

linked from the tuition page that is not publicly readable. This report does not substitute an industry figure from another
school, because such a figure would not be Cannon's price.
PUBLICATION GAP — The enrolment contract terms are not public
The tuition page refers to an enrolment contract, and the deposit is described as non-refundable, but the contract itself is not
published. That leaves several of the questions a family most needs answered before signing unavailable from the public
record: what a family owes if a student withdraws before the year begins, what is owed on a mid-year departure, whether
any part of tuition is refundable and on what schedule, what happens when a payment is late, and what the consequences
of arrears are for a student's enrolment. These are ordinary contract terms that most schools disclose at the point of
enrolment rather than on a public page, and a family should ask to see the contract before committing.
