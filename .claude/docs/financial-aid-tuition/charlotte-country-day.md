How to read this report
What this report covers
This report sets out the published price of attending Charlotte Country Day School, the costs that sit on top of

It is not financial advice and not a verdict on the school. We report published figures and name the gaps. We do not
model any family's award, we do not calculate what a family would pay after aid, and we do not tell any family what
to do.
Two framing rules applied throughout
(cid:127) Unpublished does not mean deficient. Many figures below land as ‘the school does not publish this’. That is
a transparency gap on the school's part. It is never a judgement that the school's aid programme is small, weak
or unwelcoming, and it should not be read as one.
(cid:127) Published does not mean current. Tuition resets every year and old pages linger in search results. Every
dollar figure and percentage here carries the school year it applies to. Where the school itself did not date a
figure, we say so rather than guessing.
A scope boundary worth stating plainly
This report is about K–12 tuition assistance — the money that reduces what a family pays Charlotte Country Day
School while their child is enrolled there. It is not about college financial aid, which is a separate process a family
goes through with universities after graduation. The school's own website uses the phrase ‘financial aid’ for both, so
the distinction is easy to lose.
One figure in particular is excluded for this reason. The school's 2025–26 School Profile reports $13 million in
college scholarships offered to the Class of 2025. That is money offered by universities, not by Charlotte Country
Day, and it has no bearing on what a family pays in tuition. It does not appear anywhere in the figures below.
Confirm anything decision-critical with the business office
Rates, deadlines and aid figures change annually, and several items in this report are not published anywhere
public. Section 7 ends with a list of specific questions worth putting directly to the school's business office. Anything
a family is relying on should be confirmed there.

SECTION 3 The Aid Engine — Process, Forms and Timeline
AT A GLANCE
Aid platform: Clarity · $65 fee payable on submission
Deadlines: 1 November for current families · 15 January for prospective families
Decisions released: with enrolment contracts
Renewal: awards run one year; families must re-apply annually
Decided by: the school's Financial Aid Committee, which the school states keeps all financial information strictly

This is the best-documented part of Charlotte Country Day's published cost picture. A family can establish what to
file, when to file it and what happens next without contacting the school.
Platform, documents and cost to apply
(cid:127) Applications run through Clarity. The school states the application typically takes under an hour, is
mobile-friendly, and is fully translated into Spanish, with support available in English and Spanish.
(cid:127) A $65 fee is payable at the end of the application.
(cid:127) Required documents are a 1040 income tax return, W2 forms and a recent pay stub. The school states the
tax documents are collected automatically through Clarity, so in most cases a family uploads only the pay stub.
Deadlines and how aid relates to admission
Financial aid applications and supporting documentation are due 1 November for current Country Day families
and 15 January for prospective families. The school states that decisions about awards are communicated with
enrolment contracts.
On the relationship between the two decisions, the school's admissions FAQ states plainly that admission decisions
are made separately from financial aid decisions. The school does not use the terms ‘need-blind’ or ‘need-aware’
on its public pages, so we report the school's own wording rather than translating it into a category it did not claim.
Divorced, separated and never-married parents
The school requires both custodial and non-custodial parents, regardless of legal settlements, to submit an
application through Clarity. In exceptional cases where one parent cannot comply, the custodial parent is directed to
submit a written explanation. The school states that a lack of information from either parent may prevent a family
from receiving aid.
Renewal and continuity of awards
Awards are made for one year and families are required to re-apply every year. The school states that unless there
is a significant change in annual income or available assets, families can normally expect the level of assistance to
continue from year to year, provided the student's performance meets the expectations of the Financial Aid
Committee. The school also states that awards are made without regard to race, colour, religion, sexual orientation
or ethnic origin.

THE CORE DISCLOSURE GAP IN THIS SECTION
Charlotte Country Day publishes no average award, no median award, no award range, and no statement of
whether any award covers full tuition. The published figures describe the programme's total size and reach, not the
size of an individual award.
This matters more than it might appear. A family can learn that roughly one student in five receives aid, but cannot learn

aid distributions are skewed, so a mean sits above a typical award — and here neither is available.
We have not computed an average award as a share of tuition, which this series normally reports, because there is no

Enrolment deposit: 10% of tuition, credited toward total tuition due

The school publishes its deposit rule and its three payment options clearly. What it does not publish is anything

Deposit and the aid-applicant provision
An enrolment deposit of 10% of tuition is required at the time of enrolment and is credited toward the total tuition
due, so it is a timing obligation rather than an additional charge. Families in application for financial aid pay a
reduced deposit, which the school states is refundable if the financial aid award is not substantial enough
to enable the child to attend. That provision is a meaningful piece of disclosure: it means a family can hold a
place while waiting on an aid decision without forfeiting the deposit if the answer does not work.
The school does not publish the dollar amount or percentage of the reduced deposit.

THE MIDDLE-INCOME QUESTION
Charlotte Country Day publishes nothing about families whose income sits above the threshold at which aid is typically
awarded but below comfortable full payment. There is no published sliding scale, no indexed-tuition programme, and no
guidance aimed at that group.
This is a publication gap rather than a finding about the school. Very few independent schools publish anything on this
question, and its absence here says nothing about how the school treats such families in practice.
