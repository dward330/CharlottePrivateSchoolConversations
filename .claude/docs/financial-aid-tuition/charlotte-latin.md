How to read this report
This report answers a single question for one school: what does Charlotte Latin School put on the public record
about what it costs to attend, and about the assistance it offers to families who need help meeting that cost? It
reports published figures and names the places where no figure is published.
Two rules govern everything that follows
(cid:127) An unpublished figure is not a deficiency. Where this report marks something as not published, that is a
statement about the public record and nothing more. It is not a finding that the school is weak in that area, and it
should not be read as one. Schools make different choices about what to post publicly, and many hold detailed
figures inside an enrolment portal that families see once they apply.
(cid:127) Every figure carries a year, and years change. Tuition, fees and aid figures are re-set annually and on
different cycles from one another. A figure tagged 2024-25 sitting beside one tagged 2026-27 is not a
contradiction; the two simply describe different years. Confirm anything you intend to act on directly with the
school's business office.
Scope: this report is about K-12 tuition assistance only
Two entirely separate things share the phrase financial aid, and conflating them is the most common error in this
subject area. This report covers K-12 tuition assistance — help from the school toward the cost of attending the
school. It does not cover college financial aid: the FAFSA, the CSS Profile, college merit awards, or award-letter
appeals to universities. Those belong to the college admissions process and are outside this report entirely. Where
the school's own pages use the phrase “financial aid,” this report reads it as K-12 tuition assistance unless the page
says otherwise.
What we did and did not calculate
(cid:127) Figures are reproduced exactly as published and are never rounded.
(cid:127) Every percentage is reported with the denominator the school stated. Where the school did not state one, that
is recorded as a gap rather than assumed.
(cid:127) An average and a median are different measures and are never swapped. Where the school publishes one and
not the other, this report says which one is published.
(cid:127) We never subtract aid from tuition to produce a net price. Tuition minus an average award is not a figure any
family pays.
(cid:127) Different kinds of assistance — need-based aid, endowed scholarships, discounts — are never added together
into a single total unless the school itself publishes that sum.

Why we did not publish an all-in annual cost estimate
Our standing practice is to assemble published line items into a floor and a common annual range per division,
clearly labelled as our arithmetic. We do not build that estimate when more than a third of the components are
unpriced, because a range assembled from mostly-missing parts misleads more than it helps.
Counting only components the school confirms exist, fourteen apply to a 2026-27 family. Six are priced at 2026-27.
Eight are either unpriced or published only at 2025-26 rates. That is 57 percent of components unpriced for the
current year — well past the threshold — so no all-in estimate appears in this report.

The school publishes an aim to meet up to 100% of demonstrated need and a share of students receiving some
funding. It does not publish anything about families who sit above the threshold at which aid is offered but below
comfortable full-pay — no indexed tuition programme, no middle-income initiative, no guidance on where the aid
threshold falls. That is a publication gap rather than a statement about who the school serves, and a family in that
position will get a more useful answer from a direct conversation than from any public page.
Two navigation hazards worth knowing about
(cid:127) A stale page is still circulating in search results. The school's live tuition page gives the financial aid
deadline as 15 January 2027. Search engines are still serving cached text from the same address giving 15
January 2026. A family checking the deadline through a search result can land on the older text. Open the page
directly rather than relying on the search snippet.
(cid:127) The financial aid contact address is misspelled in one place. The tuition page invites families to inquire
through an address in which the domain appears with two letters transposed, while the correct address appears
elsewhere on the same page. Mail sent to the misspelled version will not arrive. Use the address given in the
page's contact panel.
