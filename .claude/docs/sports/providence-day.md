Headline Figures (School-Reported)
Providence Day publicizes an annual “NCAA signees” graphic. A recent senior-class version states: 31
future NCAA student-athletes, 15 NCAA sports represented, 24 different colleges/universities, all divisions.
This is a single-class snapshot combining NCAA Divisions I and III — not D1 alone. [Source:
pdschargers.com homepage hero graphic]
The department also holds several signing ceremonies per year. In 2024–25: a February ceremony
recognized 7 athletes (to D-II/D-III schools), and an April ceremony added 11 more (across 10 schools),
bringing the year to 26 committed student-athletes across all divisions. A spring 2024 ceremony recognized
9 athletes across 8 sports (NCAA D-I & D-III), bringing that year to 27 committed. [Sources:
pdschargers.com February & April 2025 signing-ceremony posts; pdschargers.com Boys Track page]
Important Framing
“Committed to play college athletics” spans all three NCAA divisions plus NAIA — it is NOT “D1 only.” In the
detailed list below, true Division I commitments concentrate in football, swimming, track & field, and to a
lesser extent basketball and soccer. Baseball, lacrosse, golf, tennis, and softball send most committed
athletes to NCAA Division III programs (Davidson, Denison, Hampden-Sydney, Williams, Sewanee,
Washington & Lee, Tufts recur heavily) — a pattern matching the school's college-prep, academically
selective identity.

The NCISAA NIL Policy (Applies to Providence Day)
In February 2024, the North Carolina Independent Schools Athletic Association — which governs roughly
94–100 private schools including Providence Day — approved a policy allowing its student-athletes to profit
from their name, image, and likeness while retaining eligibility, effective the 2024–25 school year. NCISAA
Executive Director Homar Ramirez framed it as a response to college NIL: “After careful consideration it was
decided to adopt an NIL policy after watching the impact that NIL has had in college athletics.” [Sources:
HighSchoolOT “Private schools in NCISAA adopt NIL policy”; On3]
Key rules student-athletes (and PD) must follow:
(cid:127) NIL activity must be kept SEPARATE from interscholastic participation — athletes may not use the
school's name/marks or appear in school uniform in NIL deals.
(cid:127) Prohibited categories include cannabis, alcohol, gambling, adult entertainment, drugs, and tobacco.
(cid:127) All NIL agreements must be reported to and approved by the NCISAA state office.
(cid:127) School personnel (coaches, ADs) may not use NIL to recruit/enroll students or act as an athlete's agent or
marketing rep.
(cid:127) Violations can trigger a suspension (reported as up to 60 days under the policy framework).
(cid:127) [Sources: On3; Charlotte Observer (via The T&D;); Lewis Brisbois sports-law analysis]

Education then BANNED public-school NIL on June 6, 2024 — putting NC in the minority of states (at the

On the Atrium/Novant Question (Read First)
The original topic asked about official partnerships with regional healthcare giants such as Atrium Health or
Novant Health. Based on the available sources, Providence Day does NOT publicly state an Atrium or
Novant partnership. Instead, PD runs an in-house sports-medicine operation: its own staff of certified athletic
trainers plus a named team physician (Dr. Shadley Schiffern). For context, the Atrium/Novant outreach
model is common at OTHER area schools — e.g., Charlotte-Mecklenburg public schools partner with Atrium
Health, and Charlotte Country Day uses OrthoCarolina (team physician Dr. James Fleischli) — but that
framing should not be applied to Providence Day without confirmation. [Sources: pdschargers.com Athletic

Concussion Protocol
PD treats all concussions as potentially serious. The sports-medicine staff collects pre-concussion baseline
data on student-athletes in grades 7–12 using the Sport Concussion Assessment Tool (SCAT) and the
King-Devick baseline test. In compliance with North Carolina's Gfeller-Waller Concussion Awareness Act,
any athlete suspected of a concussion is removed from play and must complete a strict return-to-play
protocol before returning. [Source: pdschargers.com Athletic Training & Magnus Health page]
Strength & Conditioning Leadership
Providence Day employs full-time, CSCS-certified strength and conditioning coaches who manage
year-round physical development across the program. The school operates two weight rooms (the MAC
Wellness Center and the Overcash Field House Sports Weight Room) and its S&C; program is a five-year
recipient of the Strength of America Award (including 2021). [Source: pdschargers.com Wellness Center
page; Team Home page]

Overview
Providence Day School is a transitional-kindergarten-through-12th-grade independent, co-educational,
college-preparatory school in southeast Charlotte. Its athletics program is among the largest of any private
school in North Carolina. According to the school's Wikipedia entry, the program supports 68 teams across
the middle school, junior varsity, and varsity levels, 26 of which are varsity sports, and sports are available to
all students in grades 7 through 12. [Source: Wikipedia, “Providence Day School”]
Competition structure: Providence Day is a private school and therefore does not compete in the
public-school NCHSAA. It competes for state championships in the NCISAA. Locally, Upper School (high
school) teams play in the CISAA (Charlotte Independent School Athletic Association) against rivals such as
Charlotte Latin, Charlotte Christian, Charlotte Country Day, Cannon, and Carmel Christian; the Middle
School competes in the GCMSAA (Greater Charlotte Middle School Athletic Association). As of 2025–26,
football competes in the newly aligned NCISAA “Big South” conference alongside Charlotte Latin, Charlotte
Christian, and Charlotte Country Day. [Sources: Wikipedia, “Providence Day School”; SI/SBLive, “A closer
look at Providence Day's 2025 football schedule”]
