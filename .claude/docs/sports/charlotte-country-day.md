NIL Landscape
Research Area — name, image, and likeness context for an NCISAA school
As an NCISAA independent school, Country Day's NIL environment is governed by the relative permissiveness of
independent-school athletics rather than the NCHSAA's stricter amateurism posture — but no public Country Day NIL
deals or collectives were found.
AT A GLANCE
Governing body NCISAA (independent schools), not NCHSAA — so NCHSAA amateurism restrictions do not
directly apply.
Practical reality Country Day's athletic strengths are Olympic/racquet sports (swimming, tennis, XC), which
historically generate little high-school NIL activity.
Public deals No public NIL agreements, collectives, or figures tied to Country Day athletes were located.
Caveat Absence of public deals is not proof none exist; high-school NIL is lightly reported, especially
outside football/basketball.
Why NIL Is Muted Here
(cid:127) NIL dollars at the high-school level concentrate in football and boys' basketball with national-recruit profiles.
Country Day's marquee output is girls' swimming, tennis, and cross country — sports with limited NIL markets.
(cid:127) The school's public messaging centers on college matriculation and character, not athlete monetization.
Honest Assessment
(cid:127) No NIL collective, brand deal, or dollar figure associated with a Country Day athlete was found in public sources as
of June 2026.
(cid:127) NCISAA membership means the regulatory ceiling is higher than for NCHSAA peers, but that latent permissiveness
does not appear to have translated into reported NIL activity for this school.

National Profile — Swimming
Research Area — the flagship program's standing beyond the state
Swimming is Country Day's flagship by every research signal: a purpose-built natatorium, back-to-back state titles, the
most state records in its NCISAA division, and national All-American recognition for individual swimmers.
AT A GLANCE
Why swimming is the Dedicated $10M+ natatorium; back-to-back D1 girls state titles (2024–25); 7 of 22 NCISAA D1
flagship state records — most of any school.
National marker 13 All-American nominations from 8 swimmers in a single state meet (automatic All-American +
consideration cuts).
Pipeline Recent swim graduates have gone on to D1 programs (e.g., UC San Diego under 2016 US
Olympic women's coach David Marsh).
Honest scope National recognition here is individual All-American/Olympic-trial-aspirant level, not a national
team ranking; stated as such.
The Case for National-Caliber Swimming
(cid:127) The girls' program won three relays at the state meet, each setting a new NCISAA meet record, and went 1–2 in the
100 fly (Caroline Mallard, Taylor Klein).
(cid:127) Individual record-setters include Elle Scott (NCISAA record, 100 breast) and Caroline Mallard (NCISAA record, 100
fly).
(cid:127) USA Swimming All-American status (automatic and consideration) is a national time-standard recognition; eight
Country Day swimmers earned 13 such nominations in one season.
[Source: HighSchoolOT NCISAA D1 swimming championship coverage; Charlotte Country Day "Bucs Wrap Up" recap.]
National Context & Caveats
(cid:127) Country Day competes within the NCISAA (independent schools), so its "state" titles are NCISAA rather than
NCHSAA; national standing is best read through individual time standards and college placement rather than
national team polls.
(cid:127) No national team ranking (e.g., a published national top-25 for the team) was found; the honest national marker is
individual All-American recognition and D1-level college matriculation.
[Source: Independent review of NCISAA structure (Wikipedia NCHSAA/NCISAA); no national team-ranking source located as of June 2026.]
