Governance & Formation
The official Upper School Club List settles the formation question directly: it states that each year, students
initiate the creation of new clubs, pursuing interests beyond the classroom, and that clubs are intended to
develop student leadership, communication, and innovation. Every club pairs faculty advisor(s) with student
leaders — the consistent structural model. The list itself is explicitly described as a living document updated
through a named faculty coordinator as leadership shifts during the year. [Source: Upper School Club List

Remaining gap: while the list confirms students start clubs annually and that each needs a faculty advisor, the School does not publish
the step-by-step approval mechanics (sponsoring office, minimum membership, charter timeline). The ethos and advisor requirement are
documented; the procedure is not.

A Service-Centered Club Culture
Service is the heart of Providence Day's club life and, the School says, among its most popular. It is delivered
through the Students of Service (SOS) network — dozens of student-led service clubs engaging nearly half the
Upper School, voluntarily and without mandated hours — plus a wide set of service and mentorship clubs on the
official Upper School list. [Sources: PDS magazine — A Culture of Giving; Upper School Club List '25–26]

Beyond the broader SOS network and its named partners (Crisis Assistance Ministry, Bright Blessings, Sparks
Cards, Cards for Kindness, Key Club, Red Cross), the '25–26 Upper School list adds a wide service and

Signature Service Initiatives
School-wide efforts include the Freedom School literacy partnership for K–8 students from underserved
Charlotte communities (over a dozen students earned national recognition in 2024), the Gil Murdock Turkey
Trot food-and-supplies drive (9,757 items in 2021), an annual toy drive, and issue-based responses such as
Hurricane Helene relief drives. [Sources: providenceday.org — news; PDS magazine; alumni news]
Global Service — Round Square
As a Round Square member school, Providence Day links service to its global identity through international
service and exchange — a 2025 community-service trip to South Africa and an earlier Thailand project that
helped build a research center, park ranger huts, and lookout towers. [Source: providenceday.org — Global
Education]
Leadership, Governance & Identity
Formal governance runs through the elected Student Council and the Honor Council, with the Foundation
Board and Investment Board as additional application/election-based leadership bodies. Identity and belonging
are served by nine affinity/alliance groups — AMEMSA, AAPI, Black Student Union, Diabuddies, Fellowship of
Christian Athletes, Gender & Sexuality Alliance, Hispanic/Latinx, Jewish Culture Club, and SAGE — supported
by a full-time Chief Diversity Officer. [Sources: Upper School Club List '25–26; providenceday.org — Equity,
Inclusion, and Belonging]
Civic-Engagement Read
Providence Day's civic identity is service-forward: a large, voluntary, student-led service apparatus and a broad
affinity slate, complemented by elected Student and Honor Councils and the global-citizenship framing of the
Global Studies Diploma. The competitive civic clubs (Speech & Debate, Mock Trial, Model U.N.) add a
public-voice dimension covered in the Academic & Competitive dossier.

How Clubs Cluster Into Pathways
With the full Upper School roster confirmed, Providence Day's non-sport, non-arts activities resolve into six clear
pathways. Each is framed as exposure and demonstrated interest, not a promise of any outcome. The global
and service pathways remain the School's signature, but the official list reveals an unusually strong
business/finance pathway and a deep civic-competition pathway as well.
Pathway 1 — Global & Cultural Exposure (the Signature)
Global education remains Providence Day's defining identity. PDS built the nation's first Global Studies
Diploma, runs the TK–12 PD Passport, and is a Round Square member school with international service
(South Africa 2025; an earlier Thailand build project). The club roster feeds this directly: Model U.N. and World
Quest on the competitive side, and a deep set of language and culture clubs — Chinese, French, German,
Spanish, Italian, plus Latin Club and the Greek Society, and the International Food Club. [Sources:
providenceday.org — Global Education; Upper School Club List '25–26]

Pathway 3 — Business & Finance (a Standout)
The official list reveals a business and finance pathway unusually deep for an independent school: DECA
(competition), Future Business Leaders of America, the Investment Management Society, the Microfinance
Club, the Investment Board, and two economics competitions (Euro Challenge and the PD Economics
Challenge). For a student oriented toward business, finance, or entrepreneurship, this is a genuine multi-club
pipeline with a competitive outlet. [Source: Upper School Club List '25–26]
Pathway 4 — Civic Voice, Law & Policy
A serious civic and pre-law pathway runs through Speech & Debate, Mock Trial, Model U.N., World Quest,
and Quiz Bowl, with the Criminal Justice Club and the elected Honor Council adding law-and-governance
exposure. This cluster builds argument, evidence, public speaking, and deliberation — the skills associated with
law, government, and policy. [Source: Upper School Club List '25–26]
Pathway 5 — STEM & Innovation
The STEM pathway pairs competition with making: Science Olympiad, the Robotics Team, TSA, Drone Club,
and Build Your Own Boat on the competitive/engineering side, and AI / Machine Learning, Coding (Swift), the
Computer Science Honors Society, Science Club, and Women in STEM on the exploratory side — reinforced
by the Middle School IDEAS makerspaces. [Source: Upper School Club List '25–26]
Pathway 6 — Identity & Wellbeing
Nine affinity and alliance groups (AMEMSA, AAPI, BSU, Diabuddies, FCA, GSA, Hispanic/Latinx, JCC, SAGE)
build community and belonging — which the School identifies among its most popular organizations —
complemented by wellness clubs such as Meditation and Comfort. [Source: Upper School Club List '25–26]
Reading the Pathways Honestly
These clusters describe exposure, not outcomes. The best-validated pathways remain global and service
(dedicated programs, a first-in-nation diploma, a School-reported participation figure), but the official roster
shows the business/finance and civic-competition pathways are genuine, multi-club strengths rather than token
offerings.
